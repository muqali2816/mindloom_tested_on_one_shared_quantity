## Abstract

[The first preregistered adversarial test of consciousness theories compared two theories on three divergent predictions about the cortical signature of conscious content; each passed one criterion and failed one. We ask what the theories have committed to beyond that signature, and what they have left unmeasured. A purposive audit coded the stated predictions of 12 accounts against 10 measurable quantities (120 cells), registered 27 stated predictions and 6 derived by us and classified the content manipulated in 42 experiments; column classes were derived by script, not by a coder. The result is a concentration. Only 21 of 120 cells carry a stated prediction; 6 of 12 accounts state one about the cortical signature, the one column that is contested — of 54 cross-theory pairs of stated predictions there, 2 are discriminating, 10 jointly compatible and 42 merely different. Elsewhere commitment is sparse: the number and the incompatibility of competing action policies have one explicit occupant each, and so does visceral modulation of access; interoceptive content and autonomic conflict have two each, whose predictions do not conflict; cortical–autonomic coherence has none. The empirical base mirrors this: 18 of 30 theory-addressed experiments manipulated neutral visual content, 0 of 30 valenced content. The next adversarial test needs a condition on a quantity the theories have committed to and never measured, not another contrast on the cortical signature. We specify one — incompatibility of co-executable action policies at fixed count under no-report access — that tests two operationalisations we derived (P32, P23) from one account's stated commitments; a null bounds the derivations and refutes no theory.]{.mark}

**Keywords:** [consciousness; adversarial collaboration; conscious access; theory audit; stated predictions; action-policy competition; interoception; column typology]{.mark}

## 1. Introduction

For thirty years the standard complaint about consciousness science was that its theories were not
tested. The complaint has been answered, and the answer was worse. The Cogitate consortium ran a
preregistered adversarial test of global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) and integrated
information theory (IIT) across fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG in 256 participants, with pass and
fail criteria agreed in advance by proponents of both theories and by a theory-neutral consortium
[1], and reported that neither central prediction survived intact [2]. GNWT was challenged by the
absence of prefrontal ignition at stimulus offset — a null that cannot be blamed on insensitivity,
since offset responses were robust elsewhere and the same prefrontal sites showed strong onset
responses to the same stimuli. IIT was challenged by the absence of sustained within-posterior
synchronisation. The consortium declined a verdict, adopting a Lakatosian soft-falsification
frame, and GNWT proponents have published their own reading of the same data [3].

## 2. Earned and unearned distinctions

Any claim about consciousness is a claim about one of at least seven things, and most disputes here are about which one. The coding in Section 7 depends on holding them apart: unconscious processing; conscious content; access; online metarepresentation of one's own processing; control; post-hoc reconstruction; the putative bearer of phenomenal states. These are not degrees of one variable but dissociate pairwise.

The oldest distinction is also the least stable. Block's separation of phenomenal from access consciousness [5] made the inference from "globally available" to "felt" a step requiring argument, later given empirical teeth by the overflow claim that experience contains more than the system can report [6,7]. The programme that grew from it — isolating the correlates of conscious content from its prerequisites and consequences [8], and distinguishing conscious state from content [9] — is a genuine achievement, and the no-report literature of Section 6 its most valuable product.

What has changed is confidence in the two-kinds framing. An argument from inside the experimental camp holds that dividing consciousness into two *kinds* converts a graded multi-component phenomenon into two entities, one inaccessible to measurement by construction [10]. That is a conceptual paper, not a result, but it converges with an independent movement toward feature profiles: multidimensional assessment of animal sentience replacing a single scale [11], indicator lists for invertebrates [12], a framework treating consciousness as a profile of separately assessable capacities [13].

The field has earned the process/content/access distinctions and has *not* earned the phenomenal/access dichotomy in its strong form, and the two are usually run together to the detriment of both. The first set is earned because each distinction is independently measurable and has produced dissociations that survived attempts to explain them away: integration without awareness [14], frontal correlates tracking report rather than the percept [15], conscious state varying independently of content [9]. The dichotomy is not, because its central claim — a second kind of consciousness whose presence comes apart from access — is what no measurement can establish; overflow arguments turn on the interpretation of partial report, the opposing position denies the separation is coherent [16], an intermediate view treats access as partial and hierarchical rather than all-or-none [17].

The cost of conflating these levels shows up in how theories get evaluated. A theory specifying when content becomes globally available answers an access question, and its success says nothing directly about phenomenality; but with the dichotomy in the background such theories are read as accounts of phenomenal experience, criticised for failing a task they never took on. The converse error is as common: theories staking a claim on phenomenality are credited with the access evidence. Feature profiles help by forcing each claim to name its own observable, which is what makes the coding of Section 7 possible.

## 3. From regulation to a minimal bearer

The literature on biological self-maintenance rigorously accounts for what makes a boundary an organism's own; what it does not give, and largely does not claim to, is any route from organisation to a felt aspect.

Autopoietic theory describes a living system as a network of processes producing its own boundary, treating this closure as sufficient for "cognition" [18]. Di Paolo's refinement is decisive: bare autopoiesis is insufficient even for agency, since a system that either maintains its organisation or disintegrates has no gradations of better and worse and so no norms; what is needed is adaptivity, regulating one's own relation to viability boundaries [19]. [Neither text posits a felt aspect.]{.mark}

The corresponding corpus is basal cognition [20]. A parallel line proposes the skin-brain thesis: early nervous systems arose not as input–output machinery but to coordinate whole-body contraction [21]. That places single-body coordination at the origin of nervous systems — Section 4's starting point.

The split is over whether regulation entails experience. A moderate position keeps the functional vocabulary: cellular and bioelectric networks as problem-solvers in morphospace, the self's computational boundary set by the scale of coordination [22]. A radical position takes the step the moderate one declines, deriving sentience from cellular organisation and locating the first minds in single cells [23] — a redefinition rather than a proposal, adding no mechanism.

Three ladders locate the threshold differently. Feinberg and Mallatt place it in the Cambrian, tied to isomorphic neural representations [24]. Ginsburg and Jablonka propose unlimited associative learning as a *marker*: a detectable sign not a definition [25]. The third is homeostatic: feeling derived from regulation of the internal milieu [26], affect localised subcortically [27], a free-energy formalisation [28].

The anti-corticocentric line pushes the shared claim — the threshold is not the presence or absence of a cortex — lower than most expect. Merker places the core of the conscious state in upper brainstem and mesodiencephalon, where body schema, action space and motivational ranking integrate, cortex supplying content rather than consciousness [29]. This consolidated into the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness [30].

The transition from organisation to experience is warranted nowhere here and claimed seriously nowhere except in the radical cellular position — a strength rather than an embarrassment, since the basal cognition programme is valuable precisely because it stops short. Where the inference *is* warranted is narrower: from organisation to a *control problem* whose solution needs something the organisation does not contain. Coordinating many specialised subsystems around one body is such a problem, solved by nervous systems from their origin [21], and it is where the ladders converge. That convergence is the difficulty: because three independent programmes reach it, "integration around a single body matters" is not an available contribution. The unoccupied position is narrower — the *number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains* integrated is a measurable variable — and no ladder contains that axis, though transitions in cognitive evolution are described as changes in control regime [31].

## 4. Arbitration as a function

If conscious access exists because incompatible demands from many specialised systems must be reduced to one action of one body, the mechanisms performing that reduction are where to look. Single-body arbitration has been solved unconsciously three times over, in three formalisms, none requiring access.

The first solution is anatomical. The basal ganglia were proposed as the vertebrate solution to the selection problem: many command systems compete for one body's skeletal motor apparatus, and a centralised device is needed to resolve the conflict without being one of the competitors [32]. Formalisation showed striatal, subthalamic and pallidal microanatomy implementing an off-centre on-surround scheme [33]. This arbiter operates entirely without access [34].

The second solution is computational. Bounded accumulation of evidence to a threshold reproduces reaction-time distributions and accuracy jointly, parietal activity tracking the integral of evidence for an option [35].

The third solution is architectural. The affordance competition hypothesis holds that the visual system specifies several available actions at once while fronto-parietal circuits maintain them as mutually inhibiting population representations biased by value [36]. With two permissible movement directions, monkey dorsal premotor cortex carries two directional activity peaks simultaneously, and only after a cue is one suppressed and the other amplified to threshold [37].

Supramodular interaction theory (SIT) holds that phenomenal states exist to permit interaction among supramodular response systems — agentic, encapsulated structures defined by concerns rather than input modality — which compete for the same skeletomotor output and which, without phenomenal states, could not influence action *jointly* [38]. The passive frame formulation narrows the claim to the somatic nervous system, the mechanism passive and low-level [39].

SIT's empirical base is of three unequal kinds, stronger than its critics allow. The first is taxonomic: introspectively penetrable conflicts (pain for gain, breath holding, delay of gratification) are separated from impenetrable ones (pupillary reflex, ventriloquism and McGurk effects), the dividing line running through a skeletomotor plan. The second is experimental: participants trained to introspect rated the urge to err while sustaining compatible versus incompatible skeletomotor intentions, incompatible intentions producing stronger subjective effects while a smooth-muscle control produced none [40]. That control carries the load: without it the theory would predict a subjective effect from any antagonism. [The measure in both conditions is a rating of experienced conflict. ## 5. Valence and organism-wide reconfiguration

An arbitration account needs a common scale. The argument that incommensurable motives must become a single quantity for behaviour to be selectable, with pleasure as that currency, was made decades ago [44], and neuroeconomics gave it a neural reading as a common currency of subjective value [45]. The problem is posed *independently of theories of consciousness*, so a theory of access saying something about the scale makes a claim none of its competitors makes.

The interoceptive literature has meanwhile moved the body from monitored object to control loop, from afferent representation of homeostatic state in insula [46]. The decisive step was redescribing it as *inference* rather than readout [47], regulation proceeding by autonomic, endocrine and behavioural action bringing the body to the predicted state [48].

Organism-wide scope comes from allostasis rather than interoception as such: set points change in advance on anticipated need [49]. Functional connectivity identified a large-scale network named allostatic-interoceptive [50]. Two caveats are load-bearing: that network came from resting-state and meta-analytic methods, not causal manipulation, so it is architectural plausibility rather than demonstrated mechanism; and a recent treatment separates interoception as *modelling* the body from allostasis as *controlling* it [51].

The thesis that an emotion brings many subsystems into a compatible regime is well supported and already owned three times over. Component process theory defines emotion as synchronisation of organismic subsystems [52]. The evolutionary tradition describes emotions as operating modes tuning many parameters for a situation type [53]. Emotion psychology adds control precedence: emotion seizes control and reconfigures action readiness rather than adding content [54]; the constructionist line says the same in allostatic vocabulary [55].

The empirical base is weaker than the theoretical statements suggest. Neuroimaging meta-analysis found no category-specific brain signatures for discrete emotions [56]; meta-analysis of autonomic patterns found the same for the periphery [57]. [The two nulls do not show that global reconfiguration occurs; they show that if it occurs it takes no reproducible configuration per category. What the nulls do settle is the unit of analysis: not a category with a signature but a population of states sharing a functional task [58].]{.mark} Formal models deriving valence from regulatory dynamics rather than positing it — the rate of change of prediction error [59] — are proposals rather than results, but the closest formal analogue available.

Two refinements are non-optional. First, valence is not one parameter: hedonic "liking" and motivational "wanting" dissociate neurochemically and anatomically [60], so a one-dimensional good/bad axis coarsens what is measured. Second, controlling and experienced valence must be separated. The two-system framework holds that subcortical survival circuits produce defensive responses and bodily mobilisation while experienced fear is a separate cortical product [61]; in the stronger version, experienced emotion requires higher-order representation of the state as one's own [62], resting on the priority of metarepresentation in conscious access [63].

The LeDoux and Brown critique is correct as a constraint and overextended as a conclusion. It is correct that an integrated valent configuration can exist without being experienced, so no account may treat organism-wide valent integration as sufficient for a putative bearer of phenomenal states. It is overextended in inferring that experienced valence requires the higher-order architecture proposed: the step from "integration is not sufficient" to "metarepresentation is what is missing" is not supported by the dissociation data — the affect-first side supplies a rival subcortical mechanism [27,28]. The pain literature is the best model for keeping three levels apart. Nociception is phylogenetically broad and uncontroversial [64]; operational criteria for sentience rest not on it but on features requiring integration — motivational priority shifts, trade-offs, self-administered analgesia [65].

The judgement is that the middle level is real, measurable and under-theorised. Global defensive or appetitive reconfiguration is measurable through motivational priority and trade-off behaviour rather than report, [though it has not yet been measured as a joint cortical–autonomic state on a trial, which is the sense in which Section 7 calls the corresponding column thin,]{.mark} and is where the arbitration and valence literatures meet. What no one has done is make the *structure* of that configuration a function of the *structure* of the conflict resolved: allostatic and constructionist accounts derive affect from regulation and prediction error, neuroeconomics assumes the scale, SIT has no affective variable. The proposition that the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains sets the required dimensionality of valent integration appears unclaimed — and unlike most such propositions, it is measurable.

## 6. Theories and their empirical tests

The theories are easier to describe as a list of disagreements than of schools. Almost everyone agrees that conscious content is connected to broad availability; the disagreements are about what makes it conscious — network topology, causal structure, second-order representation, a predictive model of the body. We organise the map by dispute, where the empirical blows of the last five years landed, most striking several theories at once.

### 6.1 Where content lives

The localisation dispute — posterior versus prefrontal — was the most productive, because both sides stated mutually exclusive predictions. The workspace line ties access to late global ignition in a network privileging prefrontal cortex [66,67], its strongest evidence nonlinearity: a second attentional-blink target's fate is bimodal rather than graded [68], and the masking threshold coincides with a sharp inflection in late components [69]. Notably, GNWT's rationale for broadcast is flexibility rather than arbitration — an answer to the frame problem [70], a serial router for composite mental programmes [71]. IIT proceeds from phenomenological axioms to maximally irreducible integrated information, placing the hot zone in posterior temporo-parieto-occipital cortex [72,73], experience identified with the distinctions and relations unfolded from a substrate [74]. Recurrent processing theory holds instead that local recurrent activity in sensory cortex makes content conscious, entailing phenomenal content without access [75,76]. The dispute was long reproduced positionally [77], with the well-founded suspicion that outcomes depend on method and on who runs the experiment [78].

publications these theories are tested by or built on was classified (Table 2). The word
*purposive* is doing work. The audit is not a systematic review; there was no registered search, no
PRISMA flow and, at the time of writing, one completed coder (Section 7.2). Its tallies are counts
of coded accounts and are reported as such, never as fractions of a population of theories.]{.mark}

[The theory set was assembled under one inclusion rule and one coverage aim. The rule: an account

### 7.2 Coding procedure

[Each cell was coded with one of five codes defined in the deposited codebook (v2, frozen at
version and hash). EXPLICIT: a sentence or preregistered prediction can be quoted that names the
quantity and says what should happen to access when it varies. INTERPRETED: the prediction follows
from the stated mechanism in one nameable step but is not stated for this quantity — the code the
earlier version called IMPLICIT. NOT_LOCATED: no statement bearing on the quantity was found in the
examined sources; it is not a prediction of no effect and is never counted as one. NOT_APPLICABLE:
the theory places the quantity outside its scope, and the scope statement is quoted. UNRESOLVED:
sources of the same account conflict; the cell goes to adjudication. Every EXPLICIT and INTERPRETED
cell carries a polarity — positive, null or negative — and a null is a prediction: a stated claim
that the quantity makes no difference, or an exclusion of it from the theory's function, is coded
EXPLICIT with null polarity and reported apart from the positive cells throughout, because a
reader who sees "two accounts predict M4c" will take the two to agree that the quantity matters,
and in this table both predict that it does not. Each coded cell is anchored to a source, a locus,
an evidence status (full text or abstract only) and a coder confidence from 1 to 3. The
INTERPRETED/NOT_LOCATED boundary carries the analytic weight and we applied it conservatively, so
the count of NOT_LOCATED cells is a lower bound on the field's silence.]{.mark}

### 7.4 Reading the columns

[The class of each column was derived by the typology script after the first coding was frozen, and
we report the classes as they came out, including the three places where they contradict the

[**Contested: M8.** The cortical signature of access is stated by 6 of 12 accounts — GNWT, IIT,
recurrent processing theory, higher-order thought theory, higher-order state space theory and the
attention schema theory — and is INTERPRETED for 4 more; no account is silent on it, and the two
NOT_APPLICABLE cells (passive frame theory, unlimited associative learning) decline the level at
which the question is posed. The register holds 12 stated predictions here — the six preregistered GNWT and IIT predictions of the Cogitate protocol [1,2], the local-recurrence prediction of recurrent processing theory [75], the dorsolateral prefrontal prediction of higher-order theory [63,116], the temporo-parietal prediction of the attention schema theory [117] and the prefrontal awareness-state prediction of higher-order state space theory [82], among others — and the pair register enters all 54 cross-theory pairs of them. Most differ without conflicting: 42 pairs speak to different observables (a locus beside a timing, a decodability claim beside a connectivity claim) and 10 are jointly compatible under a shared observable. Two are discriminating. The GNWT and IIT predictions on temporal profile (P02, P05) name one observable — content-specific activation across a 0.5, 1.0 or 1.5 s stimulus — and predict values that exclude each other, an offset-locked burst against sustained activation with no offset event; this is the pair the Cogitate consortium operationalised and tested [2], and it is the pair on which the column's class rests (pair confidence 2). The second, recurrent processing theory against higher-order theory on the necessity of dorsolateral prefrontal function for awareness at matched first-order performance (P08, P09), is entered at pair confidence 1 and flagged: it discriminates only if the two sources' outcome variables — phenomenal content and metacognitive awareness — are one variable. No pair is incompatible outright. The earlier version of this review counted 15 "distinguishable" pairs here; under the four-way relation 7 of those are different, 6 jointly compatible and 2 discriminating, and the column is contested on the strength of the two, not the fifteen. The column is shared at the level of the quantity and contested at the level of one value, the temporal profile. The earlier version of this review called M8
"shared and convergent" and said that no occupant could be singled out by a measurement of it; the
register says otherwise, and so did the experiment. This is the column on which the theories have done their work: 12 of the 27 stated predictions fall here.]{.mark}

[**Single-occupant: M1 and M2.** The number of simultaneously competing policies (M1) and their
degree of incompatibility (M2) are each stated by 1 of 12 accounts, supramodular interaction
theory, whose PRISM principle makes the simultaneous arrival of incompatible inclinations at the
skeletomotor output system the trigger for conscious conflict [38,39].]{.mark}
Incompatibility there has been measured — sustaining incompatible skeletomotor intentions
while motionless produces stronger subjective effects than sustaining compatible ones [40]. [Two
features of the M1 prediction are recorded in the register (P16) and matter for Section 9. It is a
threshold, not a count effect: conscious conflict arises when two incompatible inclinations are
active at once, and the sources say nothing about more than two — a NOT_LOCATED about the further
increase, not a prediction that there is none [39]. And its outcome variable is experienced
conflict, not access to a stimulus (P17); the extension to access that Section 9 tests is our
derivation, registered as P23. Passive frame theory is INTERPRETED with null polarity on both
columns, since the field is said to present its contents whether or not conflict is present
[39,43]; predictive processing, INTERPRETED on M2 in the first pass, is NOT_LOCATED after a full-text reading of its source, which treats conflict between affordances without any statement about consciousness. No other account states or
entails a prediction over either quantity. An experiment varying incompatibility cannot
adjudicate between GNWT, IIT, recurrent processing theory or the attention schema theory, none of
which said anything about it; a positive result corroborates the supramodular prediction and
leaves the rest untouched, and the informative outcome is the null.]{.mark}

[**Occupied but not contested: M3 and M7.** Valence (M3) is stated by predictive processing at
trial level and by both origin-level accounts at lineage level, with the same sign; 5 accounts are
INTERPRETED, 4 of them with a constructed null — GNWT, higher-order thought theory, supramodular
interaction theory and passive frame theory admit valenced content and state no valence-dependent
access prediction — and the IIT cell is flagged, since its statement that affect is not an
essential property of experience can be read as a stated null rather than silence.
Metarepresentational precision (M7) is stated by higher-order thought theory, higher-order state

from first-order performance, and the register finds no pair that differs. Both columns can
corroborate or disconfirm their occupants together and cannot separate them.]{.mark}

[**Occupied but not contested, formerly called contested: M4a and M4c; single-occupant: M4b.** The earlier version of
this review named autonomic involvement as the one column carrying opposed stated predictions —
supramodular interaction theory's stated negative against the interoceptive-inference positive —
and prized it as the cell an adversarial test should want. Splitting the column into its three
questions dissolves the opposition. Access to interoceptive content as content (M4a) is stated by
predictive processing, for which visceral channels are constitutive of feeling [48], and by the
Feinberg–Mallatt scheme at lineage level [24], a cell flagged because its explicit interoceptive
category is in a source we could not read; 4 accounts are INTERPRETED positive and the neural subjective frame INTERPRETED null, since the frame is proposed as a referential not itself
experienced as content [119]. Visceral modulation of access to exteroceptive content (M4b) is stated by one account, the neural subjective frame, whose anchor is that pre-stimulus heartbeat-evoked responses predict detection of a faint grating independently of measured cardiac parameters [120] — a cell coded from the abstract and provisional until the full text is read; predictive processing is INTERPRETED, re-coded from EXPLICIT because the coded sentence reviews evidence rather than stating a prediction of the framework; 10 accounts are NOT_LOCATED. The column is single-occupant. Conscious involvement in purely
autonomic conflict (M4c) carries the two stated nulls: supramodular interaction theory predicts,
and has measured, that conflict within a smooth-muscle effector produces no experienced conflict
[40], and passive frame theory states the same boundary as scope — the conscious field serves the
somatic nervous system [39]. Both exclude autonomic conflict from the function of consciousness
and neither denies that bodily states are felt; the same text names tooth pain and urges among the
most basic conscious contents [39]. Against these nulls stand no stated positives: the
interoceptive-inference sources contain no statement about a conflict resolved by autonomic
effectors alone [47,48], the prediction that unresolved interoceptive prediction error would
yield a feeling is our derivation (P24), and the one passage bearing on it names predictions
rather than prediction errors as the likelier vehicle of conscious feeling [48]. The positives on M4a and the nulls on M4c answer different questions, no pair of stated predictions in either column is discriminating, and each is occupied but not contested; M4b, with one stated occupant, is single-occupant. Were P24
endorsed by its proponents, M4c would become the first contested column outside M8; as coded, it
is not. What has not changed is the methodological fact: a test of M4a or M4c needs a
report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content, and none has been validated — such
measures exist in the visual [15,103] and auditory [106] modalities, and cardiac interoceptive
accuracy measures require report by construction [118].]{.mark}

[**Thin, not empty: M5.** Organism-wide coherence across cortical and autonomic channels, a joint
measure of the state accompanying access, receives 0 EXPLICIT and 5 INTERPRETED cells among 12
accounts, and 7 NOT_LOCATED. M5 is a construct this review proposes, not a quantity inside any operational definition of
access in use, and the column records that no coded theory has adopted it: five are compatible with
it and none operationalises it as an organism-wide joint measure. GNWT's global availability is
organism-wide in rhetoric and cortical in measurement, its dependent variables being ignition and
P3b [121]; allostatic accounts identify a large-scale interoceptive system [50] without proposing
its coherence with cortical dynamics as an access criterion. The nearest stated prediction is the
neural subjective frame: cortical responses to heartbeats are held to be a precondition of
conscious perception [119], and pre-stimulus heartbeat-evoked responses do predict visual [120]
and somatosensory detection [122,123], as does respiratory phase [124,125]; they have also been
used to detect residual consciousness in post-comatose patients [126]. These measures couple cortex
to one visceral channel and are, by the original authors' own control analyses, independent of the
measured autonomic state [120]. They therefore index cortical monitoring of a visceral signal
rather than coherence across cortical and autonomic channels, and the programme itself frames the
quantity as necessary but not sufficient for conscious perception [119] and as distinct from
attention [127]. We code it INTERPRETED on M5 and EXPLICIT on
M4b, and we withdraw the earlier formulation that nobody operationalises a cortico-visceral
quantity as a condition on access: somebody does, single-channel, and the column is thin rather
than empty. A coder who read the heartbeat-evoked response as a cortical–autonomic coherence
metric would fill 1 of 12 cells and make the column single-occupant, and Section 7.7 carries that
sensitivity.]{.mark}

[**Occupied but not contested: M6.** Dimensionality of phenomenal space is stated by IIT, for which the structure of an experience is identical to the unfolded cause–effect structure of its substrate and therefore high-dimensional [74], and by higher-order state space theory, which nests a one-dimensional awareness state above a high-dimensional perceptual space [82]. The earlier version of this review listed the pair as distinguishable on structure and magnitude at confidence 1. The pair register enters it as *different*: the two statements address different variables — the geometry of what is experienced in the one case, the dimensionality of the judgement that marks a state as experienced in the other — and no shared observable is named under which one value would exclude the other. The column is therefore occupied but not contested, which is what the submitted version said; it returns to contested only if the first coder rules, on the sources, that the two claims concern one variable, and that ruling goes to adjudication. Three accounts are INTERPRETED, and two cells of the column are flagged, one in each direction.]{.mark}

[The columns that are single-occupant or thin intersect in a cell no study fills:]{.mark} access to valenced or interoceptive content under a
manipulated number of incompatible policies, measured across cortical and autonomic channels
jointly. Part of that cell is occupied, and by whom is worth stating precisely. Morsella and
colleagues established that policy incompatibility has measurable consequences for experience and
that smooth-muscle conflict does not [40,128]; Desender and colleagues
established the more directly relevant point that the subjective experience of conflict is a
separable measurable quantity, dissociable from conflict adaptation — adaptation occurs without
conflict awareness, and awareness of conflict has effects of its own [[129]]{.mark}, which is the closest existing precedent for treating M2 as a predictor of experience rather
than of performance; Garfinkel and colleagues, that interoceptive
accuracy dissociates from interoceptive awareness [118]. What none has done is vary conflict load
orthogonally to salience while measuring access with a report-independent signature, the
constraint the no-report literature established as necessary [102], with objections in [108].
[Nor can existing data be redescribed to fill the cell:]{.mark} no reanalysis of the Cogitate dataset can recover an
effect of policy conflict, since its stimuli — faces, objects, letters and false fonts under
target detection, hold conflict load constant by construction [2].

A gap reducible to unavailable methods is a difficulty, not a gap. Most of what the
missing measurement requires is routine, and one component is not; [the component that is not — a
report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content — was named above under M4a and M4c,
and it decides which parts of the design in Section 9 are ready to run.]{.mark} Routine: policy conflict is manipulable and its subjective consequences measurable without overt
movement [40]; report-independent access measures are established in the visual [15,103] and
auditory [106] modalities; calibrated valenced stimuli [130] and standardised cardiac interoceptive
measures [118] are off the shelf; and multi-site preregistered adversarial designs are feasible at
scale [1]. The absence is explained instead by what the theories ask for. The field's own coding shows that
support for a given theory is predictable from methodological choices alone, irrespective of

## 8. Online metarepresentation, policy candidacy and reconstruction

The upper part of the episode — registering one's own processing, treating a proposed action as one option among several, reconstructing it afterwards — [holds three dissociations that are framed but, with the partial exception conceded in Section 8.2, not measured, each conflated with a measured neighbour.]{.mark}

### 8.1 Precision, not registration

The case for treating self-registration as a separate quantity is not introspective: the accuracy of judgements about one's own processing varies separately from that processing's accuracy. Second-order signal detection supplies the apparatus, meta-d′ expressing metacognitive sensitivity in units of first-order d′ [135]; calibration, error sensitivity and performance dissociate, so "metacognition" unqualified means little [136]. [Whether theta-burst TMS to dorsolateral prefrontal cortex impairs metacognitive sensitivity with discrimination intact is disputed. Rounis and colleagues reported the impairment [116]; a replication with a modified design found none and pointed to methodological differences from the original [137]; Ruby and colleagues argued that the replication's own pre-exclusion data showed the effect and that a Bayesian re-analysis favours it [138]; Bor and colleagues replied that the prior of that re-analysis rests on data they consider invalid and that the evidence across studies does not yet demonstrate the effect [139]. Neither side has conceded, and we decide nothing here; the higher-order locus prediction in the register (Table S4, P09) carries the dispute as an auxiliary assumption.]{.mark} Lesion data implicate anterior prefrontal cortex, whose integrity predicts introspective rather than perceptual accuracy [140,141], with domain-general and domain-specific components [142] and a separate evaluative stage [143]. Registration itself grants no control. But its *precision* predicts downstream change — confidence governs information search [144], poor metacognition accompanies resistance to counter-evidence [145], symptom dimensions shift metacognitive bias without touching performance [146]. The measurable variable is metarepresentational precision, not the fact of registration — a different claim from the one most theories in Table 1 make.

Two qualifications. Self-registration may not be self-directed: confidence compares contributions to joint decisions [147], is arguably a culturally acquired skill over implicit monitoring [148], secondary to mind-reading [149], and what a workspace needs to select what is admitted [150]; the radical version makes consciousness a learned metarepresentation [151–153]. And these claims rest on non-equivalent report scales: the Perceptual Awareness Scale exists because binary seen/unseen flattens intermediate states [154] and is more sensitive to weak contents than confidence [155], the discreteness dispute is unresolved [156] against workspace theories predicting a threshold [157], and scale and quantity are poorly coordinated [158].

### 8.2 Policy candidacy

The second dissociation concerns **policy candidacy** (polyvariant choice) — the mode in which a proposed action policy ceases to be the sole command and becomes one member of a compared set. Three features define it: a change in the proposal's status, a comparison set, and independence from both online metarepresentation and the success of control.

The name requires justification, because the obvious label is taken. In computational neuroscience *meta-control* means arbitration between controllers and tuning of their parameters: model-based versus model-free learning by reliability [159], deciding how to decide [160], allocating effort by expected benefit [161], reviewed in [162]. That is parametric arbitration, not a policy represented as an object; since the term denotes the very neighbour this mode must be distinguished from, we do not use it, and this is its only appearance.

Existing paradigms measure everything adjacent to policy candidacy and not it. Meta-d′ measures the accuracy of evaluating a decision already taken [135]. The expected value of control measures the *intensity* of allocation in dorsal anterior cingulate cortex [163,164] over a cascaded prefrontal organisation [165] whose capacity limit is probably task separation, not fuel depletion [166] — the resource model having failed a preregistered replication [167] and multi-site test [168], effort recast as opportunity cost [169]. The stop-signal paradigm measures the speed of cancelling one prepared action [170,171]; its unconscious variant is decisive, masked stop signals recruiting frontal inhibitory machinery without awareness [172,173], conscious and unconscious control engaging dissociable rather than graded mechanisms [174]. Conflict detection likewise has a canonical account in which the anterior cingulate recruits control with no commitment to consciousness [175]. The closest construct is intentional inhibition, where refusal is itself a choice [176,177] — but there too no alternative is generated and compared.

A correction here strengthens the case. The first of the three quantities operationalising policy candidacy — the size of the set under consideration — *is* already measured by option generation [178,179], including its neural basis [180], so the claim that none is measurable is too strong. [The second quantity — whether a decision depends on properties of the *rejected* alternatives — has an existence proof but no measurement of the kind this section needs. The decoy effect shows that adding an asymmetrically dominated alternative, which nobody chooses, changes the choice between the two that remain [181]; the dependence therefore exists. What the decoy literature does not address, and was not designed to address, is whether the rejected alternative was accessed, whether the shift in choice depends on that access, or whether anything about the rejected option is represented as a candidate rather than as a feature of the display. The paper that established the effect says nothing about consciousness, and we cite it for the existence of the dependence only. Unmeasured, then, are the access-dependence of that effect and whether metacognitive precision predicts option-set widening rather than correctness.]{.mark} The metric for the first, **option-set widening**, should be borrowed from option generation, not reinvented; the nearest precedent for the second is that the selection threshold shifts with urgency [182].

### 8.3 Report as write-back into the self-model

The third dissociation concerns what report is evidence for. That participants report not the causes of their behaviour but plausible theories of those causes remains established, if localised more precisely than originally [183], extending to introspection about current phenomenal content [184]. Choice blindness is the strong version: when already-chosen faces were switched, many switches went unnoticed and participants justified a choice they had not made [185], reproduced outside the laboratory [186].

The result that matters most is stronger than the standard reconstruction claim: [when two of their own ratings on a moral survey were reversed by a paper-slip trick, most participants failed to detect the reversal, and about half argued for the position opposite to the one they had given moments earlier [187]. The study measured immediate acceptance and justification within the session, not the durability of the altered attitude, which its authors raise only as a possibility; the write-back claim below rests on the immediate result.]{.mark} Reconstruction is therefore not a distortion of the record but a write-back into the model of oneself — report can become a cause of the subsequent state. The same holds for the sense of agency, an inference rather than access to the motor command [188,189], and for the construction of having chosen freely [190]. Two cautions: intentional binding [191] is contested, reproduced under illusory voluntary action [192] and sensitive to phenomenological control [193]; and the Libet result must be stated exactly — the averaged readiness potential [194] is not refuted as a measurement, its causal interpretation is, since stochastic accumulation makes it an artefact of averaging [195,196]. Hence "post-hoc reconstruction" rather than "reflection": the latter imports a transparency the data do not support, itself analysed as the unavailability of the model's own modelhood to the system using it [197–199].

### 9.1 [One worked condition: incompatibility at fixed count under no-report]{.mark}

[**What is tested, and by whom it was predicted.** Study 1 tests two author-derived operationalisations (P32, P23) of one account's stated commitments and estimates a third quantity. Supramodular interaction theory states P16: conscious conflict arises when two or more incompatible inclinations toward the skeletomotor output system are active at once; nothing is stated beyond two [38,39]. The count branch does not test P16: it varies co-executable, not incompatible, policies and measures access to a masked target, not experienced conflict. P32 is therefore ours: access is higher with two or more co-executable policies than with one. P33, also ours, holds that access does not rise further from two to three — the source is silent beyond two, and silence is not a stated null — and is bounded by an equivalence test, not tested. P23 is ours as well: access rises with the incompatibility of the licensed policies at fixed count and load; the theory's stated outcome is experienced conflict [40]. None is endorsed by the theory's proponents; a null constrains our derivation, not the theory. The condition tests no stated prediction of any theory directly and discriminates between no two theories.]{.mark}

[**Design.** Three overlearned rules map a masked target's hue, position and aperture shape to three mutually distinct responses. Responses are positions of three three-position levers (each hand, dominant foot); two responses can be executed together only on different levers. Incompatibility is the number of pairs of demanded responses sharing a lever — 0, 1 or 3 — set by the effector mapping in force for the block. Effector balancing gives 11 presentation cells and 5 analysis cells: the count branch, counts 1, 2 and 3 at 0 shared pairs, and count 3 at 1 and at 3 shared pairs. Cells are blocked, not interleaved: 11 cells × 2 blocks per session, mapping fixed per block and cued at its start, order counterbalanced. On a quarter of trials a probe names one rule and the participant executes that rule alone; joint execution is never required. Whether incompatible intentions form at target onset or rules are merely held until the probe is open; probe-response latency by shared-pairs level is the manipulation check bearing on it, reported not tested.]{.mark}

[**Trial budget.** Session 1 supplies titration, a Perceptual Awareness Scale rating on every trial [154] and classifier training: 64 presented trials per presentation cell (a design choice), 704 in all, about 115 min including breaks. Session 2 supplies the confirmatory no-report data without the rating: 104 presented trials per presentation cell (26 probe, 78 no-probe), 1144 in all, about 135 min at 6 s per trial with breaks, giving 78 × 0.85 = 66.3 expected usable no-report trials per cell at an assumed 15 % loss. N = 120 (119 required for 90 % power at dz = 0.30 for one uncorrected contrast, rounded up to a multiple of the six rule-to-effector rotations), fixed in advance, no sequential monitoring (Supplementary Protocol S5).]{.mark}

[**Access index.** Trial-wise decoded evidence for "seen" from a per-participant classifier trained on the rating-labelled session-1 trials: regularised linear discriminant analysis on 100–500 ms post-target amplitudes at 17 parieto-occipital channels of a 64-channel montage, rating binarised as 1 against 2–4, nested cross-validation with block-wise folds, specification to be preregistered and frozen before session 2 is scored [103,200–202]. The index is EEG-only. It is a candidate marker of access, not a measure of M5; because a no-report participant may covertly categorise [102,108], every contrast is also computed on the session-1 rating.]{.mark}

[**Autonomic recording.** ECG, skin conductance and pupil are recorded throughout, with a jittered 2.5–3.5 s inter-trial interval and deconvolution of overlapping responses. A cortical–autonomic coupling index (the per-participant slope of decoded access on pre-stimulus heartbeat-evoked amplitude; its trial-wise correlation with the phasic autonomic responses) is reported with estimate and interval per cell as estimation only; no coded theory predicts over it.]{.mark}

[**Contrasts, multiplicity and power.** Two confirmatory contrasts, Holm-corrected at family-wise α = 0.05: C0 tests P32, count 1 against the mean of counts 2 and 3 (weights −1, ½, ½) at 0 shared pairs; C1a tests P23, 0 shared pairs against the mean of 1 and 3 at count 3. An equivalence test of count 3 against 2 bounds P33 within ±0.30 dz by two one-sided tests [203]; C1b, a trend linear in shared pairs and a two-degree-of-freedom omnibus, is exploratory. A contrast counts as supported only if its EEG-index and PAS-report versions are both significant after correction, with the same sign. Each confirmatory contrast singly has uncorrected power 0.90 at N = 120 and dz = 0.30; at least 0.84 after Holm correction (the Bonferroni level; 0.89 if the two p-values were independent); for the conjunctive rule the product under independence is 0.71 to 0.80, to be revised from the pilot correlation between versions. Equivalence power within ±0.30 dz at N = 120 is 0.895.]{.mark}

[**Mandatory pilot.** Three criteria, thresholds to be preregistered in S5, must be met before the confirmatory sample runs: the masking staircase holds visibility inside its target band; the classifier, trained at one incompatibility level, reaches AUC ≥ 0.70 at each other level — a validity test defining the population to which the result applies (awareness decodable at that accuracy) without selecting for the hypothesised effect; and full-contrast accuracy on the single probed rule reaches criterion in every cell. Failure on any criterion stops the study; the pilot is reported regardless.]{.mark}

[**Valence.** Study 2 repeats the design with negative, neutral and positive content, testing negative against positive at matched arousal. This tests P34, our derivation, not the stated P13, which concerns experienced valence and free-energy change, not sad against happy access. Pupil and skin conductance are manipulation checks for arousal matching and enter only a sensitivity analysis as covariates, being possible consequences of the manipulation. The incompatibility × valence interaction is estimated, not tested, for want of power (S5). A valence effect would corroborate our derivation and separate none of the coded theories.]{.mark}

### 9.2 [The condition that cannot yet be run, and what would make it adversarial]{.mark}

[The earlier version of this review presented an autonomic-only condition — a conflict resolved
wholly by autonomic effectors, with posture fixed and respiratory pattern change blocked — as the
most discriminating test in principle, because it would set supramodular interaction theory's
stated null against an interoceptive-inference positive. Section 7.4 has withdrawn the second
half of that opposition: the interoceptive-inference sources state nothing about autonomic-only
conflict, and the positive is our derivation (P24). The condition therefore tests, as things
stand, two stated nulls and one derivation, and becomes adversarial only if proponents of
interoceptive inference endorse P24 or state a prediction of their own. We have named the
letter-to-proponents route in Section 7.7 as the next check; this is the cell it would most
directly affect.]{.mark}

The design difficulty is unchanged and we flag it rather than leave it for a reviewer. Most
homeostatic crises recruit skeletal effectors — respiration through the diaphragm and
intercostals, thermoregulation through shivering and posture, so breath-holding is not evidence
against a skeletomotor boundary. What is required is a conflict whose resolution is wholly
autonomic: antagonism between thermoregulatory and gastrointestinal demands with posture fixed and
respiratory pattern change blocked, or orthostatic and thermal loading of the baroreflex without
postural correction. De-efferentation (high spinal injury, locked-in syndrome) is not a discriminator either, since
the claim concerns intentions rather than execution and the central skeletomotor plan is
preserved. Blocking respiratory pattern change is itself a skeletomotor intervention and does not
escape the objection: in an awake participant, skeletomotor intentions survive any fixation of
posture or breathing, and supramodular interaction theory is a claim about intentions.

[The deeper obstacle is the one named in Section 7.4: the condition requires a report-independent
measure of access to interoceptive content — a signature tracking, trial by trial, whether an
interoceptive event was accessed, validated against report in a calibration set, generalising to
no-report blocks, and dissociable from afferent strength, arousal and attention to the body, which
alone modulates the heartbeat-evoked potential [204,205] — and no such measure has been
validated. The order of work is therefore the reverse of the order of interest. The condition of
Section 9.1 uses established visual no-report measures and can be run now; it tests two author-derived operationalisations (P32, P23) of one account's stated commitments and estimates a third quantity, and its informative outcome for the derivations is a bounded
null. The autonomic condition would need both a validation study and a stated prediction from the
other side before it could adjudicate anything. That is the actual conclusion of this review, and
it is a smaller claim than an adversarial test on a new quantity — but it names both the
measurement and the statement whose absence keeps the cell closed.]{.mark} Presenting [the autonomic condition]{.mark} as ready would be the kind
of overclaim this review has been criticising.

A closing note on what this review has and has not done. We have not proposed a theory. We have
[documented, by coding, that the field's stated predictions are concentrated on one quantity and
that most of the remaining quantities are single-occupant, thin or occupied by accounts that do
not disagree, and specified the experiment built on the one stated prediction the audit found
outside that quantity, which tests two author-derived operationalisations of it rather than the prediction itself.]{.mark} A theory that does make predictions about the [single-occupant
and thin coordinates]{.mark} —
about the format in which incommensurable local evaluations become comparable, its dimensionality
as a function of the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains, and organism-wide
reconfiguration breadth as a second condition on access, exists and is set out separately, as
Arbitration Format Theory, in a companion paper. It does not propose an alternative physical basis
for consciousness, and its claims are stated over observables rather than over experience. Whether
it is right is a separate question from whether the [columns are single-occupant or thin. They are so]{.mark} either way,
and the experiment that [operationalises the one stated prediction among them]{.mark} is worth running whoever turns out to have predicted the answer.
