## Abstract

[The first preregistered adversarial test of consciousness theories compared two theories on three predictions about the cortical signature of conscious content: on the temporal profile they excluded each other's value and were separated; on the other two each failed its own criterion. We ask what the theories have committed to beyond that signature, and what they have left unmeasured. A purposive audit coded the stated predictions of 12 accounts against 10 measurable quantities (120 cells), registered 27 stated predictions and classified the content of 32 publications; column classes follow by rule from coded relations between predictions, not from a coder's judgement. The result is a concentration: only 21 of 120 cells carry a stated prediction; 6 of 12 accounts state one about the cortical signature, the one contested column — of 54 cross-theory pairs of stated predictions there, 2 are discriminating. Elsewhere commitment is sparse: the number and the incompatibility of competing action policies and visceral modulation of access have one explicit occupant each; interoceptive content three and autonomic conflict two, none in conflict; cortical–autonomic coherence none. The empirical base mirrors this: 16 of 23 theory-addressed publications manipulated neutral visual content, 0 of 23 valenced content. The next adversarial test needs a condition on a quantity the theories have committed to and never measured. We specify one: incompatibility of licensed policies at fixed count under no-report access; one confirmatory contrast tests a one-step derivation (same condition, different outcome) from a stated prediction also tested on its own variable.]{.mark}

**Keywords:** [consciousness; adversarial collaboration; conscious access; theory audit; stated predictions; action-policy competition; interoception; column typology]{.mark}

## 1. Introduction

[For thirty years the standard complaint about consciousness science was that its theories were not tested. The complaint has been answered, and the answer was worse. The Cogitate consortium ran a preregistered adversarial test of global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) and integrated information theory (IIT) across fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG in 256 participants, with pass and fail criteria agreed in advance by proponents of both theories and by a theory-neutral consortium [1], and reported that neither central prediction survived intact [2] — GNWT's principal failure, the absent prefrontal ignition at stimulus offset, being a null not attributable to insensitivity, since the same prefrontal sites showed strong onset responses to the same stimuli. The consortium declined a verdict, adopting a Lakatosian soft-falsification frame, and GNWT proponents have published their own reading of the same data [3].]{.mark}

[The usual lesson drawn is about the theories: too vague, too unfalsifiable, too well defended by auxiliary assumptions. We draw a different one, and not the one we drew in the version of this review first submitted, which held that the two theories had been tested on a prediction they share. That was wrong: the consortium preregistered three predictions on which GNWT and IIT diverge, and each theory passed at least one criterion and failed at least one [1,2] (Section 6.4). The test discriminated — on a single quantity, the cortical signature of conscious content — and the consortium's remark that its challenges extend to any theory sharing the predictions tested [2] is a remark about how many theories have staked their commitments on that one quantity.]{.mark}

[That is the thesis of this review: the stated commitments of theories of consciousness concentrate on a few measurable quantities, and most of what the theories could be tested on is unmeasured — not because the measurements are unavailable but because no theory has said what it expects. Only a contested column offers a result that favours one occupant over another, and silence is not a contrary prediction: an experiment on a single-occupant or thin column adjudicates between none of the silent theories, and its informative outcome is the null. Which quantity falls into which class is the question Section 7 answers; Section 9 draws the design consequence.]{.mark}

Locating such a quantity requires knowing what the theories actually predict, as opposed to what

## 2. Earned and unearned distinctions

Any claim about consciousness is a claim about one of at least seven things, and most disputes here are about which one. The coding in Section 7 depends on holding them apart: unconscious processing; conscious content; access; online metarepresentation of one's own processing; control; post-hoc reconstruction; the putative bearer of phenomenal states. These are not degrees of one variable but dissociate pairwise.

The oldest distinction is also the least stable. Block's separation of phenomenal from access consciousness [5] made the inference from "globally available" to "felt" a step requiring argument, later given empirical teeth by the overflow claim that experience contains more than the system can report [6,7]. The programme that grew from it — isolating the correlates of conscious content from its prerequisites and consequences [8], and distinguishing conscious state from content [9] — is a genuine achievement, and the no-report literature of Section 6 its most valuable product.

What has changed is confidence in the two-kinds framing. An argument from inside the experimental camp holds that dividing consciousness into two *kinds* converts a graded multi-component phenomenon into two entities, one inaccessible to measurement by construction [10]. That is a conceptual paper, not a result, but it converges with an independent movement toward feature profiles: multidimensional assessment of animal sentience replacing a single scale [11], indicator lists for invertebrates [12], a framework treating consciousness as a profile of separately assessable capacities [13].

The field has earned the process/content/access distinctions and has *not* earned the phenomenal/access dichotomy in its strong form, and the two are usually run together to the detriment of both. The first set is earned because each distinction is independently measurable and has produced dissociations that survived attempts to explain them away: integration without awareness [14], frontal correlates tracking report rather than the percept [15], conscious state varying independently of content [9]. The dichotomy is not, because its central claim — a second kind of consciousness whose presence comes apart from access — is what no measurement can establish; overflow arguments turn on the interpretation of partial report, the opposing position denies the separation is coherent [16], an intermediate view treats access as partial and hierarchical rather than all-or-none [17].

The cost of conflating these levels shows up in how theories get evaluated. A theory specifying when content becomes globally available answers an access question, and its success says nothing directly about phenomenality; but with the dichotomy in the background such theories are read as accounts of phenomenal experience, criticised for failing a task they never took on. The converse error is as common: theories staking a claim on phenomenality are credited with the access evidence. Feature profiles help by forcing each claim to name its own observable, which is what makes the coding of Section 7 possible.

## 3. From regulation to a minimal bearer

The literature on biological self-maintenance rigorously accounts for what makes a boundary an organism's own; what it does not give, and largely does not claim to, is any route from organisation to a felt aspect.

Autopoietic theory describes a living system as a network of processes producing its own boundary, treating this closure as sufficient for "cognition" [18]. Di Paolo's refinement is decisive: bare autopoiesis is insufficient even for agency, since a system that either maintains its organisation or disintegrates has no gradations of better and worse and so no norms; what is needed is adaptivity, regulating one's own relation to viability boundaries [19]. [Neither text posits a felt aspect.]{.mark}

The corresponding corpus is basal cognition [20]. A parallel line proposes the skin-brain thesis: early nervous systems arose not as input–output machinery but to coordinate whole-body contraction [21]. That places single-body coordination at the origin of nervous systems — Section 4's starting point.

The split is over whether regulation entails experience. A moderate position keeps the functional vocabulary: cellular and bioelectric networks as problem-solvers in morphospace, the self's computational boundary set by the scale of coordination [22]. A radical position takes the step the moderate one declines, deriving sentience from cellular organisation and locating the first minds in single cells [23] — a redefinition rather than a proposal, adding no mechanism.

Three ladders locate the threshold differently. Feinberg and Mallatt place it in the Cambrian, tied to isomorphic neural representations [24]. Ginsburg and Jablonka propose unlimited associative learning as a *marker*: a detectable sign not a definition [25]. The third is homeostatic: feeling derived from regulation of the internal milieu [26], affect localised subcortically [27], a free-energy formalisation [28].

The anti-corticocentric line pushes the shared claim — the threshold is not the presence or absence of a cortex — lower than most expect. Merker places the core of the conscious state in upper brainstem and mesodiencephalon, where body schema, action space and motivational ranking integrate, cortex supplying content rather than consciousness [29]. This consolidated into the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness [30].

The transition from organisation to experience is warranted nowhere here and claimed seriously nowhere except in the radical cellular position — a strength rather than an embarrassment, since the basal cognition programme is valuable precisely because it stops short. Where the inference *is* warranted is narrower: from organisation to a *control problem* whose solution needs something the organisation does not contain. Coordinating many specialised subsystems around one body is such a problem, solved by nervous systems from their origin [21], and it is where the ladders converge. That convergence is the difficulty: because three independent programmes reach it, "integration around a single body matters" is not an available contribution. The unoccupied position is narrower — the *number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains* integrated is a measurable variable — and no ladder contains that axis, though transitions in cognitive evolution are described as changes in control regime [31].

## 4. Arbitration as a function

If conscious access exists because incompatible demands from many specialised systems must be reduced to one action of one body, the mechanisms performing that reduction are where to look. Single-body arbitration has been solved unconsciously three times over, in three formalisms, none requiring access.

The first solution is anatomical. The basal ganglia were proposed as the vertebrate solution to the selection problem: many command systems compete for one body's skeletal motor apparatus, and a centralised device is needed to resolve the conflict without being one of the competitors [32]. Formalisation showed striatal, subthalamic and pallidal microanatomy implementing an off-centre on-surround scheme [33]. This arbiter operates entirely without access [34].

The second solution is computational. Bounded accumulation of evidence to a threshold reproduces reaction-time distributions and accuracy jointly, parietal activity tracking the integral of evidence for an option [35].

The third solution is architectural. The affordance competition hypothesis holds that the visual system specifies several available actions at once while fronto-parietal circuits maintain them as mutually inhibiting population representations biased by value [36]. With two permissible movement directions, monkey dorsal premotor cortex carries two directional activity peaks simultaneously, and only after a cue is one suppressed and the other amplified to threshold [37].

Supramodular interaction theory (SIT) holds that phenomenal states exist to permit interaction among supramodular response systems — agentic, encapsulated structures defined by concerns rather than input modality — which compete for the same skeletomotor output and which, without phenomenal states, could not influence action *jointly* [38]. The passive frame formulation narrows the claim to the somatic nervous system, the mechanism passive and low-level [39].

SIT's empirical base is of three unequal kinds, stronger than its critics allow. The first is taxonomic: introspectively penetrable conflicts (pain for gain, breath holding, delay of gratification) are separated from impenetrable ones (pupillary reflex, ventriloquism and McGurk effects), the dividing line running through a skeletomotor plan. The second is experimental: participants trained to introspect rated the urge to err while sustaining compatible versus incompatible skeletomotor intentions, incompatible intentions producing stronger subjective effects while a smooth-muscle control produced none [40]. That control carries the load: without it the theory would predict a subjective effect from any antagonism. [The measure in both conditions is a rating of experienced conflict. It shows that incompatible skeletomotor intentions are accompanied by a reportable state; it does not show that the arbitration required that state, nor that the state is conscious access to anything beyond the conflict itself. Arbitration is demonstrated by the outcome of the competition, access by a separate measure, and the experiment supplies the first and reports the second. Section 9 accordingly treats the step from experienced conflict to access as a derivation of ours, labelled as such, rather than as a claim of the theory.]{.mark} The third support is neuroimaging and markedly weaker: subjective conflict was associated with left postcentral, that is somatosensory rather than executive, cortex [41]. First, the three unconscious solutions establish decisively that policy competition is *insufficient* for access: competition is present in all three and access is absent. They establish nothing about its necessity. ## 5. Valence and organism-wide reconfiguration

An arbitration account needs a common scale. The argument that incommensurable motives must become a single quantity for behaviour to be selectable, with pleasure as that currency, was made decades ago [44], and neuroeconomics gave it a neural reading as a common currency of subjective value [45]. The problem is posed *independently of theories of consciousness*, so a theory of access saying something about the scale makes a claim none of its competitors makes.

The interoceptive literature has meanwhile moved the body from monitored object to control loop, from afferent representation of homeostatic state in insula [46]. The decisive step was redescribing it as *inference* rather than readout [47], regulation proceeding by autonomic, endocrine and behavioural action bringing the body to the predicted state [48].

Organism-wide scope comes from allostasis rather than interoception as such: set points change in advance on anticipated need [49]. Functional connectivity identified a large-scale network named allostatic-interoceptive [50]. Two caveats are load-bearing: that network came from resting-state and meta-analytic methods, not causal manipulation, so it is architectural plausibility rather than demonstrated mechanism; and a recent treatment separates interoception as *modelling* the body from allostasis as *controlling* it [51].

The thesis that an emotion brings many subsystems into a compatible regime is well supported and already owned three times over. Component process theory defines emotion as synchronisation of organismic subsystems [52]. The evolutionary tradition describes emotions as operating modes tuning many parameters for a situation type [53]. Emotion psychology adds control precedence: emotion seizes control and reconfigures action readiness rather than adding content [54]; the constructionist line says the same in allostatic vocabulary [55].

The empirical base is weaker than the theoretical statements suggest. Neuroimaging meta-analysis found no category-specific brain signatures for discrete emotions [56]; meta-analysis of autonomic patterns found the same for the periphery [57]. [The two nulls do not show that global reconfiguration occurs; they show that if it occurs it takes no reproducible configuration per category. The evidence that it occurs is the evidence of the previous paragraph — synchronisation of subsystems, control precedence, operating modes — and that evidence is theoretical statement and behavioural observation rather than a measured organism-wide state. What the nulls do settle is the unit of analysis: not a category with a signature but a population of states sharing a functional task [58].]{.mark} Formal models deriving valence from regulatory dynamics rather than positing it — the rate of change of prediction error [59] — are proposals rather than results, but the closest formal analogue available.

Two refinements are non-optional. First, valence is not one parameter: hedonic "liking" and motivational "wanting" dissociate neurochemically and anatomically [60], so a one-dimensional good/bad axis coarsens what is measured. Second, controlling and experienced valence must be separated. The two-system framework holds that subcortical survival circuits produce defensive responses and bodily mobilisation while experienced fear is a separate cortical product [61]; in the stronger version, experienced emotion requires higher-order representation of the state as one's own [62], resting on the priority of metarepresentation in conscious access [63].

The LeDoux and Brown critique is correct as a constraint and overextended as a conclusion. It is correct that an integrated valent configuration can exist without being experienced, so no account may treat organism-wide valent integration as sufficient for a putative bearer of phenomenal states. It is overextended in inferring that experienced valence requires the higher-order architecture proposed: the step from "integration is not sufficient" to "metarepresentation is what is missing" is not supported by the dissociation data — the affect-first side supplies a rival subcortical mechanism [27,28]. The pain literature is the best model for keeping three levels apart. Nociception is phylogenetically broad and uncontroversial [64]; operational criteria for sentience rest not on it but on features requiring integration — motivational priority shifts, trade-offs, self-administered analgesia [65].

The judgement is that the middle level is real, measurable and under-theorised. Global defensive or appetitive reconfiguration is measurable through motivational priority and trade-off behaviour rather than report, [though it has not yet been measured as a joint cortical–autonomic state on a trial, which is the sense in which Section 7 calls the corresponding column thin,]{.mark} and is where the arbitration and valence literatures meet. What no one has done is make the *structure* of that configuration a function of the *structure* of the conflict resolved: allostatic and constructionist accounts derive affect from regulation and prediction error, neuroeconomics assumes the scale, SIT has no affective variable. The proposition that the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains sets the required dimensionality of valent integration appears unclaimed — and unlike most such propositions, it is measurable.

## 6. Theories and their empirical tests

The theories are easier to describe as a list of disagreements than of schools. Almost everyone agrees that conscious content is connected to broad availability; the disagreements are about what makes it conscious — network topology, causal structure, second-order representation, a predictive model of the body. We organise the map by dispute, where the empirical blows of the last five years landed, most striking several theories at once.

### 6.1 Where content lives

The localisation dispute — posterior versus prefrontal — was the most productive, because both sides stated mutually exclusive predictions. The workspace line ties access to late global ignition in a network privileging prefrontal cortex [66,67], its strongest evidence nonlinearity: a second attentional-blink target's fate is bimodal rather than graded [68], and the masking threshold coincides with a sharp inflection in late components [69]. Notably, GNWT's rationale for broadcast is flexibility rather than arbitration — an answer to the frame problem [70], a serial router for composite mental programmes [71]. IIT proceeds from phenomenological axioms to maximally irreducible integrated information, placing the hot zone in posterior temporo-parieto-occipital cortex [72,73], experience identified with the distinctions and relations unfolded from a substrate [74]. Recurrent processing theory holds instead that local recurrent activity in sensory cortex makes content conscious, entailing phenomenal content without access [75,76]. The dispute was long reproduced positionally [77], with the well-founded suspicion that outcomes depend on method and on who runs the experiment [78].

### 6.3 Access without report

Dependence on report biases toward frontal theories, and the key result is direct: in binocular rivalry, frontal activity tracks introspection and the act of reporting rather than the percept [15]. ## 8. Online metarepresentation, policy candidacy and reconstruction

The upper part of the episode — registering one's own processing, treating a proposed action as one option among several, reconstructing it afterwards — [holds three dissociations that are framed but, with the partial exception conceded in Section 8.2, not measured, each conflated with a measured neighbour.]{.mark}

### 8.1 Precision, not registration

[The accuracy of judgements about one's own processing varies separately from that processing's accuracy: meta-d′ expresses metacognitive sensitivity in units of first-order d′ [135], and calibration, error sensitivity and performance dissociate [136]. Whether theta-burst TMS to dorsolateral prefrontal cortex impairs metacognitive sensitivity with discrimination intact is disputed [137–140]; we decide nothing here, and the higher-order locus prediction in the register (Table S4, P09) carries the dispute as an auxiliary assumption. Lesion data implicate anterior prefrontal cortex, whose integrity predicts introspective rather than perceptual accuracy [141,142], with domain-general and domain-specific components [143] and a separate evaluative stage [144]. Registration grants no control, but its *precision* predicts downstream change — information search [145], resistance to counter-evidence [146], metacognitive bias shifting with symptom dimensions while performance stands [147]. The measurable variable is metarepresentational precision, not the fact of registration, which is not the claim most theories in Table 1 make. Two qualifications: self-registration may not be self-directed — confidence compares contributions to joint decisions [148], may be a culturally acquired skill [149] secondary to mind-reading [150] or a workspace's admission criterion [151], and in the radical version consciousness is a learned metarepresentation [152–154]; and the report scales are not equivalent — the Perceptual Awareness Scale exists because binary seen/unseen flattens intermediate states [155] and is more sensitive than confidence [156], the discreteness dispute is unresolved [157] against workspace theories predicting a threshold [158], and scale and quantity are poorly coordinated [159].]{.mark}

### 8.2 Policy candidacy

[**Policy candidacy** (polyvariant choice) is the mode in which a proposed action policy ceases to be the sole command and becomes one member of a compared set: a change in the proposal's status, a comparison set, and independence from both online metarepresentation and the success of control. The obvious label, *meta-control*, is taken — in computational neuroscience it means arbitration between controllers and tuning of their parameters [160–163], parametric arbitration rather than a policy represented as an object — and we do not use it.]{.mark}

[Existing paradigms measure everything adjacent to policy candidacy and not it. Meta-d′ measures the evaluation of a decision already taken [135]. The expected value of control measures the *intensity* of allocation in dorsal anterior cingulate cortex [164,165] over a cascaded prefrontal organisation [166] whose limit is task separation, not fuel depletion [167] — the resource model failed a preregistered replication [168] and a multi-site test [169], and effort is recast as opportunity cost [170]. The stop-signal paradigm measures the speed of cancelling one prepared action [171,172], which masked signals achieve without awareness [173,174] through mechanisms dissociable from conscious control [175]; conflict detection recruits control with no commitment to consciousness [176]; intentional inhibition, where refusal is itself a choice [177,178], is the closest construct, but no alternative is generated and compared there either.]{.mark}

[A correction strengthens the case. The first quantity operationalising policy candidacy — the size of the set under consideration — *is* measured by option generation [179–181], so the claim that none is measurable is too strong, and the metric for **option-set widening** should be borrowed from there. The second — whether a decision depends on properties of the *rejected* alternatives — has an existence proof but no measurement of the kind needed: the decoy effect shows that an asymmetrically dominated alternative nobody chooses changes the choice between the two that remain [182], but not whether the rejected option was accessed or whether the shift depends on that access; we cite it for the existence of the dependence only. The selection threshold shifting with urgency [183] is the nearest precedent for a threshold measure. Unmeasured, then, are the access-dependence of the decoy effect and whether metacognitive precision predicts option-set widening rather than correctness.]{.mark}

### 8.3 Report as write-back into the self-model

[Participants report not the causes of their behaviour but plausible theories of those causes [184], including about current phenomenal content [185]; in choice blindness, switched faces went unnoticed and participants justified a choice they had not made [186,187]. Stronger still: when two of their own moral-survey ratings were reversed by a paper-slip trick, most participants failed to notice and about half argued for the opposite position moments later [188] — an immediate result, not a measure of the altered attitude's durability, and the write-back claim rests on the immediate result. Reconstruction is therefore not a distortion of the record but a write-back into the model of oneself: report can become a cause of the subsequent state. The same holds for the sense of agency, an inference rather than access to the motor command [189,190], and for the construction of having chosen freely [191]. Two cautions: intentional binding [192] is contested, reproduced under illusory voluntary action [193] and sensitive to phenomenological control [194]; and the Libet result must be stated exactly — the averaged readiness potential [195] is not refuted as a measurement, its causal interpretation is, since stochastic accumulation makes it an artefact of averaging [196,197]. Hence "post-hoc reconstruction" rather than "reflection", which imports a transparency the data do not support — itself analysed as the unavailability of the model's own modelhood to the system using it [198–200].]{.mark}


### 8.4 [What is measured and what is not]{.mark}

[The three dissociations do not stand in the same place, and the ledger is uneven rather than empty. Metarepresentational precision has validated instruments — meta-d′ and its calibration variants — and no experiment that asks whether precision, rather than registration, gates access; that test can be run with what exists. Policy candidacy is measured in part: option generation already counts the set under consideration (Section 8.2), and the decoy effect shows that a decision can depend on properties of a rejected alternative, whereas whether that dependence runs through access to the rejected option has no measurement at all. Write-back has been demonstrated [188] but has not been turned into a measure that any theory is committed to. What the three share is therefore not absence of measurement but absence of a claimant: none is the dependent variable any theory in Table 1 predicts, and Section 7 documents what that costs. Each would be cheaper to test than the localisation dispute that has absorbed the field's resources; whether any of them is tested depends on whether some theory is prepared to put a prediction on it.]{.mark}

## 9. Conclusion: the minimal measurement

The field's problem is not a shortage of theories or of rigour. [It is that the coded theories concentrate their stated predictions on one of ten measurable quantities — the cortical signature of access, where one pair of stated predictions, on temporal profile, is discriminating and where the first adversarial test accordingly separated its two targets — and have stated almost nothing about the other nine: 12 of the 27 stated predictions in the register fall in that column, and the rest of the space is not contested but, for the most part, unspoken for. Section 7.4 says what each other column can carry; two of its results drive the design: the number and incompatibility of competing action policies are single-occupant, stated by supramodular interaction theory alone and confined by construction to skeletal muscle [38,39], and in the checked sample of theory-addressed experiments, under the stated attribution rule, valenced content is absent.]{.mark}

[One experiment converts part of that description into a result, and we state exactly which part. Hold salience, task relevance and the number of distinguishable responses fixed. Vary how many of the responses the content demands share an effector and so cannot be executed together. Measure access with a report-independent signature, and record autonomic channels alongside it. In the report session, have the participant rate the experienced conflict itself, in the theory's own variable, after each probed response. In a separate branch, kept as an estimate, vary how many responses are demanded at all, with none sharing an effector.]{.mark}

[The design replaces the earlier version's because of a confound that design carried: incompatibility was set by the target's feature conjunction — three rules demanding three identical keys, two identical and one different, or three different keys — so the number of distinct responses rose with incompatibility and any effect could have been attributed to response-set size or congruency instead. The supramodular sources locate the gradation of conscious conflict along the compatibility axis, not the number axis — compatible plans may co-occur without any perturbation of consciousness [39] — so the earlier design could not have tested what the theory states. Here the target always demands three distinct responses, one per rule, and only the effector mapping in force for the block decides whether they can be executed jointly; the number of policies is varied only in the separate branch at zero incompatibility, where a load account — GNWT joined to a capacity assumption it has not made, registered as the derived P25 — would predict a decrement and the authors' derived P32 an increase.]{.mark}

### 9.1 [One worked condition: incompatibility at fixed count under no-report]{.mark}

[**What is tested, and by whom it was predicted.** We specify one: incompatibility of licensed policies at fixed count under no-report access; one confirmatory contrast tests a one-step derivation (same condition, different outcome) from a stated prediction also tested on its own variable. The stated prediction is supramodular interaction theory's P16 — conscious conflict at two or more incompatible skeletomotor inclinations, nothing stated beyond two [38,39]. The derivation is P23, ours, one step from P16: the same condition (incompatible intentions), a different outcome (access to the masked target that licenses them, instead of experienced conflict [40]); a null bounds our derivation, not the theory. P16 itself is tested on its own variable — the experienced-conflict rating of Morsella et al. [40] — by a separately preregistered report contrast (P35). The count branch is estimation: P32 (an access threshold at two or more co-executable policies) and P25 (a load decrement), our derivations, have opposite signs; P33 (no further rise from two to three) is a bounded estimate. It is kept because the nuisance account "more levers, more access" predicts opposite signs on the two contrasts, so its estimate says whether lever number explains the confirmatory result.]{.mark}

[**Design.** Three overlearned rules map a masked target's hue, position and aperture shape to three distinct responses on three three-position levers; responses combine only across levers. Incompatibility — 0, 1 or 3 pairs of demanded responses sharing a lever — is set by the mapping in force for the block. Seven presentation cells, five analysis cells: count 1 and count 2 at 0 shared pairs (one cell each, lever rotated between participants); count 3 at 0, at 1 (three pooled cells, one per rule pair sharing a lever) and at 3 shared pairs. Cells are blocked, mapping fixed per block: 16 blocks per session, two per cell and three in the two single-cell incompatibility cells. The third block does not equalise precision — about 99 usable no-report trials against about 199 in the pooled cell — but removes the floor risk and roughly halves the gap. Each session opens with a titration phase; the contrast is then frozen for all its blocks, nothing adapts within blocks, and between-session drift is reported. A probe on a quarter of trials names one rule, executed alone; joint execution is never required. In session 1 only, after each probe response — never before, which would disrupt execution and latency — the participant rates the urge to err on the 8-point scale of Morsella et al. [40].]{.mark}

[**Trial budget.** Presented and usable trials per cell, session durations (session 2 about 105 min, one visit) and the fixed N = 120 (119 required at dz = 0.30 for one uncorrected contrast, rounded to a multiple of the six lever rotations; no sequential monitoring) are derived in Supplementary Protocol S5.]{.mark}

[**Access index and autonomic recording.** Trial-wise decoded evidence for "seen" from a per-participant classifier trained on the rating-labelled session-1 no-probe trials: regularised linear discriminant analysis on 100–500 ms post-target amplitudes at 17 parieto-occipital channels, Perceptual Awareness Scale rating [155] binarised as 1 against 2–4, nested block-wise cross-validation, specification frozen before session 2 is scored [103,201–203]. The index is EEG-only, a candidate marker of access, not a measure of M5; because a no-report participant may covertly categorise [102,108], the confirmatory contrast is also computed on the session-1 rating. ECG, skin conductance and pupil are recorded throughout; a cortical–autonomic coupling index is reported per cell with estimate and interval, estimation only: no coded theory predicts over it.]{.mark}

[**Contrasts and power.** One confirmatory contrast on the access index, C1a, tests P23: 0 shared pairs against the mean of 1 and 3 at count 3 (weights −1, ½, ½), α = 0.05 two-sided, no multiplicity correction; it is supported only if its EEG-index and PAS-report versions are both significant in the predicted direction. Power at N = 120 is 0.90 at dz = 0.30, 0.82 for the two-version rule under independence. CR tests the stated P16 on the session-1 rating, same cells and weights, in its own preregistered family, at an assumed dz = 0.50 — under a third of the only anchor, dz = 1.66 on the same rating [40] — with power above 0.99 (0.90 at dz = 0.30). C0 (count 1 against the mean of 2 and 3 at 0 shared pairs) is an estimate with two-sided interval, its sign read against P32 and P25; the count-3 minus count-2 difference is reported with the narrowest equivalence bound it satisfies [204], P33 being supported only inside ±0.30 dz. C1b (linear trend, two-df omnibus) is exploratory.]{.mark}

[**Mandatory pilot.** Four criteria, preregistered in S5, must be met before the confirmatory sample runs: visibility inside its band after titration and under the frozen contrast; classifier AUC ≥ 0.70 across incompatibility levels — a validity test that defines the population addressed without selecting for the hypothesised effect; probed-rule accuracy at criterion in every cell; and a pilot urge-rating difference between the 3-shared and 0-shared cells of at least dz = 0.50, the value assumed for CR, below which that test is unwarranted and the manipulation, not the hypothesis, has failed. Failure on any criterion stops the study; the pilot is reported regardless.]{.mark}

[**Valence.** Study 2 repeats the design with negative, neutral and positive content, testing negative against positive at matched arousal — P34, our derivation, not the stated P13, which concerns experienced valence and free-energy change. Pupil and skin conductance, possible consequences of the manipulation, are arousal-matching checks and sensitivity-analysis covariates only; the incompatibility × valence interaction is estimated, not tested (S5). A valence effect would corroborate our derivation and separate no coded theory.]{.mark}

### 9.2 [The condition that cannot yet be run, and what would make it adversarial]{.mark}

[The earlier version presented an autonomic-only condition — a conflict resolved wholly by autonomic effectors — as the most discriminating test in principle, setting supramodular interaction theory's stated null against an interoceptive-inference positive; Section 7.4 has withdrawn the second half of that opposition, the positive being our derivation (P24). As things stand the condition tests two stated nulls and one derivation; it becomes adversarial, and M4c the first contested column outside M8, only if proponents of interoceptive inference endorse P24 or state a prediction of their own — the cell the letter-to-proponents route of Section 7.7 would most directly affect.]{.mark}

The design difficulty is unchanged and we flag it rather than leave it for a reviewer. Most
homeostatic crises recruit skeletal effectors — respiration through the diaphragm and
intercostals, thermoregulation through shivering and posture, so breath-holding is not evidence
against a skeletomotor boundary. What is required is a conflict whose resolution is wholly
autonomic: antagonism between thermoregulatory and gastrointestinal demands with posture fixed and
respiratory pattern change blocked, or orthostatic and thermal loading of the baroreflex without
postural correction. De-efferentation (high spinal injury, locked-in syndrome) is not a discriminator either, since
the claim concerns intentions rather than execution and the central skeletomotor plan is
preserved. Blocking respiratory pattern change is itself a skeletomotor intervention and does not
escape the objection: in an awake participant, skeletomotor intentions survive any fixation of
posture or breathing, and supramodular interaction theory is a claim about intentions.

[The deeper obstacle is the one component of the missing measurement that is not routine (Section 7.4): a test of M4a or M4c requires a report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content — a signature tracking, trial by trial, whether an interoceptive event was accessed, validated against report in a calibration set, generalising to no-report blocks, and dissociable from afferent strength, arousal and attention to the body, which alone modulates the heartbeat-evoked potential [205,206]. The visual and auditory measures of Section 7.4 do not transfer, cardiac interoceptive accuracy measures require report by construction [116], and none has been validated for interoceptive content. The order of work is therefore the reverse of the order of interest. The condition of Section 9.1 uses established visual no-report measures and can be run now; the informative outcome for the derivation it tests (P23) is a bounded null, and it carries a direct report-based test of the stated P16 alongside. The autonomic condition would need both a validation study and a stated prediction from the other side before it could adjudicate anything. That is the actual conclusion of this review — a smaller claim than an adversarial test on a new quantity, but one that names both the measurement and the statement whose absence keeps the cell closed.]{.mark} Presenting [the autonomic condition]{.mark} as ready would be the kind
of overclaim this review has been criticising.

A closing note on what this review has and has not done. We have not proposed a theory. We have
[documented, by coding, the concentration with which this section opened, and specified the experiment built on the one stated prediction the audit found outside that quantity, which tests a one-step derivation of it under no-report access and the prediction itself on its own report variable.]{.mark} A theory that does make predictions about the [single-occupant
and thin coordinates]{.mark} —
about the format in which incommensurable local evaluations become comparable, its dimensionality
as a function of the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains, and organism-wide
reconfiguration breadth as a second condition on access, exists and is set out separately, as
Arbitration Format Theory, in a companion paper. It does not propose an alternative physical basis
for consciousness, and its claims are stated over observables rather than over experience. Whether
it is right is a separate question from whether the [columns are single-occupant or thin. They are so]{.mark} either way,
and the experiment that [operationalises the one stated prediction among them]{.mark} is worth running whoever turns out to have predicted the answer.
