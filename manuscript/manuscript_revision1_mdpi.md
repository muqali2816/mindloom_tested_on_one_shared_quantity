## Abstract

[The first large preregistered adversarial test in consciousness science produced no winner. The Cogitate consortium challenged central predictions of global neuronal workspace theory and integrated information theory without adjudicating between them, noting that the challenges extend to any theory sharing those predictions. The outcome is structural: theories are compared almost exclusively on where and when conscious contents are encoded, so one design tests many and discriminates none. A purposive audit coded the stated predictions of a set of theories against eight measurable quantities and classified the content manipulated in 36 studies these theories are tested by or built on. Three kinds of column differ. A shared column, the cortical signature of access, carries convergent predictions from most coded theories; it is what Cogitate tested. A contested column, autonomic channel involvement, carries opposed predictions — supramodular interaction theory denies what interoceptive-inference accounts assert — but cannot yet be run, because no report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content exists. The number and incompatibility of competing action policies are single-occupant columns, predicted by one theory and stated by no other; no reclassification of borderline cells makes either contested. Organism-wide cortical–autonomic coherence is thin rather than empty: no coded theory states a prediction over it, three are compatible with one, and heartbeat-evoked responses supply the nearest single-channel operationalisation. Among theory-addressed studies neutral visual content dominates; no coded theory claims the valenced or interoceptive literature. The experiment that can be run now tests a single-occupant prediction; its informative outcome is the null.]{.mark}

## 1. Introduction

For thirty years the standard complaint about consciousness science was that its theories were not
tested. The complaint has been answered, and the answer was worse. The Cogitate consortium ran a
preregistered adversarial test of global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) and integrated
information theory (IIT) across fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG in 256 participants, with pass and
fail criteria agreed in advance by proponents of both theories and by a theory-neutral consortium
[1], and reported that neither central prediction survived intact [2]. GNWT was challenged by the
absence of prefrontal ignition at stimulus offset — a null that cannot be blamed on insensitivity,
since offset responses were robust elsewhere and the same prefrontal sites showed strong onset
responses to the same stimuli. IIT was challenged by the absence of sustained within-posterior
synchronisation. The consortium declined a verdict, adopting a Lakatosian soft-falsification
frame, and GNWT proponents have published their own reading of the same data [3].

The usual lesson drawn is about the theories: too vague, too unfalsifiable, too well defended by
auxiliary assumptions. The more consequential lesson is about the measurements, and the consortium
points at it: the prefrontal prediction is shared by those higher-order theories that treat
prefrontal cortex as a supplier of visual content rather than merely as what makes content
conscious, and the posterior prediction is shared by recurrent processing theory. An adversarial
test, on this reading, does not punish a theory; it punishes a *class of prediction*. A theory
whose distinctive prediction happens to be the one everyone else also makes cannot be
distinguished by the experiment that tests it, however well that experiment is run.

That is the thesis of this review. The problem is not that theories of consciousness are untested;
it is that they predict the same quantity and are therefore tested by the same experiment. [If the
diagnosis is right, the remedy is not another adversarial collaboration on the same dependent
variable. But the remedy is not simply a quantity the theories do not predict, and two senses in
which a measurable quantity can be a gap have to be kept apart, because they license different
inferences. A column is *contested* when at least two live theories state opposed predictions over
it; only an experiment varying such a quantity discriminates, since one side must lose. A column is

adjudicate between none of the silent theories, because silence is not a contrary prediction, and

## 2. Earned and unearned distinctions

Any claim about consciousness is a claim about one of at least seven things, and most disputes here are about which one. The coding in Section 7 depends on holding them apart: unconscious processing; conscious content; access; online metarepresentation of one's own processing; control; post-hoc reconstruction; the putative bearer of phenomenal states. These are not degrees of one variable but dissociate pairwise.

The oldest distinction is also the least stable. Block's separation of phenomenal from access consciousness [5] made the inference from "globally available" to "felt" a step requiring argument, later given empirical teeth by the overflow claim that experience contains more than the system can report [6,7]. The programme that grew from it — isolating the correlates of conscious content from its prerequisites and consequences [8], and distinguishing conscious state from content [9] — is a genuine achievement, and the no-report literature of Section 6 its most valuable product.

What has changed is confidence in the two-kinds framing. An argument from inside the experimental camp holds that dividing consciousness into two *kinds* converts a graded multi-component phenomenon into two entities, one inaccessible to measurement by construction [10]. That is a conceptual paper, not a result, but it converges with an independent movement toward feature profiles: multidimensional assessment of animal sentience replacing a single scale [11], indicator lists for invertebrates [12], a framework treating consciousness as a profile of separately assessable capacities [13].

The field has earned the process/content/access distinctions and has *not* earned the phenomenal/access dichotomy in its strong form, and the two are usually run together to the detriment of both. The first set is earned because each distinction is independently measurable and has produced dissociations that survived attempts to explain them away: integration without awareness [14], frontal correlates tracking report rather than the percept [15], conscious state varying independently of content [9]. The dichotomy is not, because its central claim — a second kind of consciousness whose presence comes apart from access — is what no measurement can establish; overflow arguments turn on the interpretation of partial report, the opposing position denies the separation is coherent [16], an intermediate view treats access as partial and hierarchical rather than all-or-none [17].

The cost of conflating these levels shows up in how theories get evaluated. A theory specifying when content becomes globally available answers an access question, and its success says nothing directly about phenomenality; but with the dichotomy in the background such theories are read as accounts of phenomenal experience, criticised for failing a task they never took on. The converse error is as common: theories staking a claim on phenomenality are credited with the access evidence. Feature profiles help by forcing each claim to name its own observable, which is what makes the coding of Section 7 possible.

## 3. From regulation to a minimal bearer

The literature on biological self-maintenance rigorously accounts for what makes a boundary an organism's own; what it does not give, and largely does not claim to, is any route from organisation to a felt aspect.

Autopoietic theory describes a living system as a network of processes producing its own boundary, treating this closure as sufficient for "cognition" [18]. Di Paolo's refinement is decisive: bare autopoiesis is insufficient even for agency, since a system that either maintains its organisation or disintegrates has no gradations of better and worse and so no norms; what is needed is adaptivity, regulating one's own relation to viability boundaries [19]. [Neither text posits a felt aspect.]{.mark}

The corresponding corpus is basal cognition [20]. A parallel line proposes the skin-brain thesis: early nervous systems arose not as input–output machinery but to coordinate whole-body contraction [21]. That places single-body coordination at the origin of nervous systems — Section 4's starting point.

The split is over whether regulation entails experience. A moderate position keeps the functional vocabulary: cellular and bioelectric networks as problem-solvers in morphospace, the self's computational boundary set by the scale of coordination [22]. A radical position takes the step the moderate one declines, deriving sentience from cellular organisation and locating the first minds in single cells [23] — a redefinition rather than a proposal, adding no mechanism.

Three ladders locate the threshold differently. Feinberg and Mallatt place it in the Cambrian, tied to isomorphic neural representations [24]. Ginsburg and Jablonka propose unlimited associative learning as a *marker*: a detectable sign not a definition [25]. The third is homeostatic: feeling derived from regulation of the internal milieu [26], affect localised subcortically [27], a free-energy formalisation [28].

The anti-corticocentric line pushes the shared claim — the threshold is not the presence or absence of a cortex — lower than most expect. Merker places the core of the conscious state in upper brainstem and mesodiencephalon, where body schema, action space and motivational ranking integrate, cortex supplying content rather than consciousness [29]. This consolidated into the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness [30].

The transition from organisation to experience is warranted nowhere here and claimed seriously nowhere except in the radical cellular position — a strength rather than an embarrassment, since the basal cognition programme is valuable precisely because it stops short. Where the inference *is* warranted is narrower: from organisation to a *control problem* whose solution needs something the organisation does not contain. Coordinating many specialised subsystems around one body is such a problem, solved by nervous systems from their origin [21], and it is where the ladders converge. That convergence is the difficulty: because three independent programmes reach it, "integration around a single body matters" is not an available contribution. The unoccupied position is narrower — the *number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains* integrated is a measurable variable — and no ladder contains that axis, though transitions in cognitive evolution are described as changes in control regime [31].


## 4. Arbitration as a function

If conscious access exists because incompatible demands from many specialised systems must be reduced to one action of one body, the mechanisms performing that reduction are where to look. Single-body arbitration has been solved unconsciously three times over, in three formalisms, none requiring access.

The first solution is anatomical. The basal ganglia were proposed as the vertebrate solution to the selection problem: many command systems compete for one body's skeletal motor apparatus, and a centralised device is needed to resolve the conflict without being one of the competitors [32]. Formalisation showed striatal, subthalamic and pallidal microanatomy implementing an off-centre on-surround scheme [33]. This arbiter operates entirely without access [34].

The second solution is computational. Bounded accumulation of evidence to a threshold reproduces reaction-time distributions and accuracy jointly, parietal activity tracking the integral of evidence for an option [35].

The third solution is architectural. The affordance competition hypothesis holds that the visual system specifies several available actions at once while fronto-parietal circuits maintain them as mutually inhibiting population representations biased by value [36]. With two permissible movement directions, monkey dorsal premotor cortex carries two directional activity peaks simultaneously, and only after a cue is one suppressed and the other amplified to threshold [37].

Supramodular interaction theory (SIT) holds that phenomenal states exist to permit interaction among supramodular response systems — agentic, encapsulated structures defined by concerns rather than input modality — which compete for the same skeletomotor output and which, without phenomenal states, could not influence action *jointly* [38]. The passive frame formulation narrows the claim to the somatic nervous system, the mechanism passive and low-level [39].

SIT's empirical base is of three unequal kinds, stronger than its critics allow. The first is taxonomic: introspectively penetrable conflicts (pain for gain, breath holding, delay of gratification) are separated from impenetrable ones (pupillary reflex, ventriloquism and McGurk effects), the dividing line running through a skeletomotor plan. The second is experimental: participants trained to introspect rated the urge to err while sustaining compatible versus incompatible skeletomotor intentions, incompatible intentions producing stronger subjective effects while a smooth-muscle control produced none [40]. That control carries the load: without it the theory would predict a subjective effect from any antagonism. The third support is neuroimaging and markedly weaker: subjective conflict was associated with left postcentral, that is somatosensory rather than executive, cortex [41]. The sharpest question is Clark's: did voluntary skeletomotor action arise for control of the external environment or the internal one, with voluntary regulation of internal-state conflicts as a source of visceral awareness [42].

Our judgement has two parts. First, the three unconscious solutions establish decisively that policy competition is *insufficient* for access: competition is present in all three and access is absent. They establish nothing about its necessity. Necessity would require showing that access does not arise without policy competition, and nothing reviewed here bears on that; §9 accordingly treats it as an untested discriminating prediction. Any theory claiming that access exists *for* arbitration therefore owes an account of what distinguishes the arbitration requiring access from the arbitration that does not; pointing at the existence of conflict is not that account.

Second, SIT does supply such an account, and it is *taxonomic* rather than computational: the conflict either sits at the action-selection stage and involves skeletal musculature, or it does not. This is the strongest available answer, and it pays a price: the classifying feature says where the conflict is located but not what makes it unresolvable without access, so the explanation cannot transfer without re-running the taxonomy. A related cost attaches to the passive frame formulation: the conscious field presents contents but does not select, and operates whether or not conflict is present [43]. But a frame passive with respect to selection has no variable that can differ between arbitration problems, as a discriminating prediction requires. SIT buys defensibility with a commitment hard to extend, and the cell where an extension would be tested — non-skeletal conflict — is where it makes a clear negative prediction.

## 5. Valence and organism-wide reconfiguration

An arbitration account needs a common scale. The argument that incommensurable motives must become a single quantity for behaviour to be selectable, with pleasure as that currency, was made decades ago [44], and neuroeconomics gave it a neural reading as a common currency of subjective value [45]. The problem is posed *independently of theories of consciousness*, so a theory of access saying something about the scale makes a claim none of its competitors makes.

The interoceptive literature has meanwhile moved the body from monitored object to control loop, from afferent representation of homeostatic state in insula [46]. The decisive step was redescribing it as *inference* rather than readout [47], regulation proceeding by autonomic, endocrine and behavioural action bringing the body to the predicted state [48].

Organism-wide scope comes from allostasis rather than interoception as such: set points change in advance on anticipated need [49]. Functional connectivity identified a large-scale network named allostatic-interoceptive [50]. Two caveats are load-bearing: that network came from resting-state and meta-analytic methods, not causal manipulation, so it is architectural plausibility rather than demonstrated mechanism; and a recent treatment separates interoception as *modelling* the body from allostasis as *controlling* it [51].

The thesis that an emotion brings many subsystems into a compatible regime is well supported and already owned three times over. Component process theory defines emotion as synchronisation of organismic subsystems [52]. The evolutionary tradition describes emotions as operating modes tuning many parameters for a situation type [53]. Emotion psychology adds control precedence: emotion seizes control and reconfigures action readiness rather than adding content [54]; the constructionist line says the same in allostatic vocabulary [55].

The empirical base is weaker than the theoretical statements suggest. Neuroimaging meta-analysis found no category-specific brain signatures for discrete emotions [56]; meta-analysis of autonomic patterns found the same for the periphery [57]. Global reconfiguration therefore *occurs* but takes no reproducible configuration per category, so the useful unit is a population of states sharing a functional task [58]. Formal models deriving valence from regulatory dynamics rather than positing it — the rate of change of prediction error [59] — are proposals rather than results, but the closest formal analogue available.

Two refinements are non-optional. First, valence is not one parameter: hedonic "liking" and motivational "wanting" dissociate neurochemically and anatomically [60], so a one-dimensional good/bad axis coarsens what is measured. Second, controlling and experienced valence must be separated. The two-system framework holds that subcortical survival circuits produce defensive responses and bodily mobilisation while experienced fear is a separate cortical product [61]; in the stronger version, experienced emotion requires higher-order representation of the state as one's own [62], resting on the priority of metarepresentation in conscious access [63].

The LeDoux and Brown critique is correct as a constraint and overextended as a conclusion. It is correct that an integrated valent configuration can exist without being experienced, so no account may treat organism-wide valent integration as sufficient for a putative bearer of phenomenal states. It is overextended in inferring that experienced valence requires the higher-order architecture proposed: the step from "integration is not sufficient" to "metarepresentation is what is missing" is not supported by the dissociation data — the affect-first side supplies a rival subcortical mechanism [27,28]. The pain literature is the best model for keeping three levels apart. Nociception is phylogenetically broad and uncontroversial [64]; operational criteria for sentience rest not on it but on features requiring integration — motivational priority shifts, trade-offs, self-administered analgesia [65].

The judgement is that the middle level is real, measurable and under-theorised. Global defensive or appetitive reconfiguration is measurable through motivational priority and trade-off behaviour rather than report, and is where the arbitration and valence literatures meet. What no one has done is make the *structure* of that configuration a function of the *structure* of the conflict resolved: allostatic and constructionist accounts derive affect from regulation and prediction error, neuroeconomics assumes the scale, SIT has no affective variable. The proposition that the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains sets the required dimensionality of valent integration appears unclaimed — and unlike most such propositions, it is measurable.

## 6. Theories and their empirical tests

The theories are easier to describe as a list of disagreements than of schools. Almost everyone agrees that conscious content is connected to broad availability; the disagreements are about what makes it conscious — network topology, causal structure, second-order representation, a predictive model of the body. We organise the map by dispute, where the empirical blows of the last five years landed, most striking several theories at once.

### 6.1 Where content lives

The localisation dispute — posterior versus prefrontal — was the most productive, because both sides stated mutually exclusive predictions. The workspace line ties access to late global ignition in a network privileging prefrontal cortex [66,67], its strongest evidence nonlinearity: a second attentional-blink target's fate is bimodal rather than graded [68], and the masking threshold coincides with a sharp inflection in late components [69]. Notably, GNWT's rationale for broadcast is flexibility rather than arbitration — an answer to the frame problem [70], a serial router for composite mental programmes [71]. IIT proceeds from phenomenological axioms to maximally irreducible integrated information, placing the hot zone in posterior temporo-parieto-occipital cortex [72,73], experience identified with the distinctions and relations unfolded from a substrate [74]. Recurrent processing theory holds instead that local recurrent activity in sensory cortex makes content conscious, entailing phenomenal content without access [75,76]. The dispute was long reproduced positionally [77], with the well-founded suspicion that outcomes depend on method and on who runs the experiment [78].

### 6.3 Access without report

Dependence on report biases toward frontal theories, and the key result is direct: in binocular rivalry, frontal activity tracks introspection and the act of reporting rather than the percept [15]. The programmatic statement [102] and the isolation of perceptual from report-related correlates [103] extend to a report-independent bifurcation signature [104], large-scale networks in a no-report visual paradigm [105] and audition [106]. Calibration is needed both ways: report reliably adds frontal correlates, but null frontal results do not themselves bury prefrontal theories [107], and no-report designs have their own defect — without report one does not know what the participant experienced [108].

### 6.4 What Cogitate removed

Exactness matters, because the loose version circulates widely. For IIT the most direct challenge was the absence of sustained within-posterior synchronisation; its content-maintenance prediction passed the pre-set criteria — sustained responses reflecting stimulus duration appeared in occipital and lateral temporal cortex — but sustained representation of orientation did not, although orientation decoded reliably in all three modalities. For GNWT the principal failure was the absence of ignition at stimulus offset, with a secondary challenge on content: category decoded in prefrontal cortex independently of task, identity not at all, orientation only in MEG — raising the question whether prefrontal cortex broadcasts all conscious content or only an abstract part [2].

The adversarial test succeeded methodologically and failed theoretically, and that combination is the most important fact currently available about the field. It succeeded where it matters: predictions fixed in advance, criteria agreed by opponents, results reported against them rather than reinterpreted afterwards. It failed in that the outcome was not fewer live theories but less confidence in a shared dependent variable. The structural lesson is the one the consortium states and the field has not absorbed: a prediction shared with competitors is no asset, because the experiment testing it tests everyone at once. What a new theory can most usefully offer is a prediction about a quantity current designs hold constant — and whether such a quantity exists is answerable by coding the theories.

## 8. Online metarepresentation, policy candidacy and reconstruction

The upper part of the episode — registering one's own processing, treating a proposed action as one option among several, reconstructing it afterwards — [holds three dissociations that are framed but, with the partial exception conceded in Section 8.2, not measured, each conflated with a measured neighbour.]{.mark}

### 8.1 Precision, not registration

The case for treating self-registration as a separate quantity is not introspective: the accuracy of judgements about one's own processing varies separately from that processing's accuracy. Second-order signal detection supplies the apparatus, meta-d′ expressing metacognitive sensitivity in units of first-order d′ [133]; calibration, error sensitivity and performance dissociate, so "metacognition" unqualified means little [134]. Theta-burst TMS to prefrontal cortex impairs metacognitive awareness with discrimination intact [135]; lesion data implicate anterior prefrontal cortex, whose integrity predicts introspective rather than perceptual accuracy [136,137], with domain-general and domain-specific components [138] and a separate evaluative stage [139]. Registration itself grants no control. But its *precision* predicts downstream change — confidence governs information search [140], poor metacognition accompanies resistance to counter-evidence [141], symptom dimensions shift metacognitive bias without touching performance [142]. The measurable variable is metarepresentational precision, not the fact of registration — a different claim from the one most theories in Table 1 make.

Two qualifications. Self-registration may not be self-directed: confidence compares contributions to joint decisions [143], is arguably a culturally acquired skill over implicit monitoring [144], secondary to mind-reading [145], and what a workspace needs to select what is admitted [146]; the radical version makes consciousness a learned metarepresentation [147–149]. And these claims rest on non-equivalent report scales: the Perceptual Awareness Scale exists because binary seen/unseen flattens intermediate states [150] and is more sensitive to weak contents than confidence [151], the discreteness dispute is unresolved [152] against workspace theories predicting a threshold [153], and scale and quantity are poorly coordinated [154].

### 8.2 Policy candidacy

The second dissociation concerns **policy candidacy** (polyvariant choice) — the mode in which a proposed action policy ceases to be the sole command and becomes one member of a compared set. Three features define it: a change in the proposal's status, a comparison set, and independence from both online metarepresentation and the success of control.

The name requires justification, because the obvious label is taken. In computational neuroscience *meta-control* means arbitration between controllers and tuning of their parameters: model-based versus model-free learning by reliability [155], deciding how to decide [156], allocating effort by expected benefit [157], reviewed in [158]. That is parametric arbitration, not a policy represented as an object; since the term denotes the very neighbour this mode must be distinguished from, we do not use it, and this is its only appearance.

Existing paradigms measure everything adjacent to policy candidacy and not it. Meta-d′ measures the accuracy of evaluating a decision already taken [133]. The expected value of control measures the *intensity* of allocation in dorsal anterior cingulate cortex [159,160] over a cascaded prefrontal organisation [161] whose capacity limit is probably task separation, not fuel depletion [162] — the resource model having failed a preregistered replication [163] and multi-site test [164], effort recast as opportunity cost [165]. The stop-signal paradigm measures the speed of cancelling one prepared action [166,167]; its unconscious variant is decisive, masked stop signals recruiting frontal inhibitory machinery without awareness [168,169], conscious and unconscious control engaging dissociable rather than graded mechanisms [170]. Conflict detection likewise has a canonical account in which the anterior cingulate recruits control with no commitment to consciousness [171]. The closest construct is intentional inhibition, where refusal is itself a choice [172,173] — but there too no alternative is generated and compared.

A correction here strengthens the case. The first of the three quantities operationalising policy candidacy — the size of the set under consideration — *is* already measured by option generation [174,175], including its neural basis [176], so the claim that none is measurable is too strong. Unmeasured are whether a decision depends on properties of the *rejected* alternatives, and whether metacognitive precision predicts option-set widening rather than correctness. The metric for the first, **option-set widening**, should be borrowed from option generation, not reinvented; the nearest precedent for the second is that the selection threshold shifts with urgency [177].

### 8.3 Report as write-back into the self-model

The third dissociation concerns what report is evidence for. That participants report not the causes of their behaviour but plausible theories of those causes remains established, if localised more precisely than originally [178], extending to introspection about current phenomenal content [179]. Choice blindness is the strong version: when already-chosen faces were switched, many switches went unnoticed and participants justified a choice they had not made [180], reproduced outside the laboratory [181].

The result that matters most is stronger than the standard reconstruction claim: switching participants' own answers in a moral survey shifted the attitudes themselves, durably [182]. Reconstruction is therefore not a distortion of the record but a write-back into the model of oneself — report can become a cause of the subsequent state. The same holds for the sense of agency, an inference rather than access to the motor command [183,184], and for the construction of having chosen freely [185]. Two cautions: intentional binding [186] is contested, reproduced under illusory voluntary action [187] and sensitive to phenomenological control [188]; and the Libet result must be stated exactly — the averaged readiness potential [189] is not refuted as a measurement, its causal interpretation is, since stochastic accumulation makes it an artefact of averaging [190,191]. Hence "post-hoc reconstruction" rather than "reflection": the latter imports a transparency the data do not support, itself analysed as the unavailability of the model's own modelhood to the system using it [192–194].

Policy candidacy is measured in part: option generation already counts the set under consideration (Section 8.2), whereas the dependence of a decision on properties of the *rejected* alternatives has no measurement at all. Write-back has been demonstrated [182] but has not been turned into a measure that any theory is committed to. What the three share is therefore not absence of measurement but absence of a claimant: none is the dependent variable any theory in Table 1 predicts, and Section 7 documents what that costs. Each would be cheaper to test than the localisation dispute that has absorbed the field's resources; whether any of them is tested depends on whether some theory is prepared to put a prediction on it.]{.mark}

## 9. Conclusion: the minimal measurement

The field's problem is not a shortage of theories or of rigour. [It is that the coded theories
concentrate their stated predictions on one of eight measurable quantities, so a well-run
adversarial experiment tests many of them simultaneously and none distinctively.]{.mark} That is what
Cogitate demonstrated, and the consortium said so: the challenges extend to theories sharing the
predictions tested [2]. [Two coding results locate the columns that
are not shared, and the classification of Section 7.4 says what each can carry. The number and
incompatibility of competing action policies are single-occupant columns: supramodular interaction
theory predicts over both and confines the prediction to skeletal muscle by construction [38,39], and no other coded account states a prediction over either.
Organism-wide coherence across cortical and autonomic channels is stated by no coded theory and is
compatible with three. The one contested column is autonomic channel involvement, where two
families have opposed predictions on record. And in the theory-addressed literature the valenced
and interoceptive studies are absent because no coded theory claims them, not because they do not
exist.]{.mark}

One experiment converts the gap into a result. Hold salience and task relevance fixed. Vary the
number of mutually incompatible action policies that the content licenses, across at least three
levels. Run it once with neutral and once with valenced content matched on salience. Measure
access with a report-independent signature alongside a simultaneous autonomic channel.

The design needs one control that the coding scheme makes obvious and that a naive version would
omit. [Increasing the number of mutually incompatible policies also increases cognitive load, and
load degrades access under every theory in Table 1. GNWT in particular, joined to any capacity
assumption, predicts that several competing policies consume workspace resources and so degrade
access; that is a prediction, and Table 1 does not code GNWT against it, because it is a
prediction about load rather than about the policies' incompatibility. What GNWT and the other
coded theories do not predict is an effect of *incompatibility at matched load*. The design
therefore holds the number of candidate policies, their salience and the working-memory demand
constant and varies only whether the policies are mutually exclusive: the same option set, the
same load, incompatibility present or reduced to zero. The supramodular prediction is an effect of
incompatibility with policy count fixed; a pure capacity account predicts an effect of count with
incompatibility fixed. Only the crossing of the two licenses a claim about conflict.]{.mark} Three
quantities from Table 1 then become jointly observable for the first time, and each of the three
outcomes is informative in a different way.

### 9.1 One worked condition: incompatibility × valence under no-report

[**Design and participants.** A within-subject 3 × 3 crossing of the incompatibility of the action policies the content licenses (I1, I2, I3) with content valence (negative, neutral, positive), policy count fixed at three, plus a neutral-only load-control arm varying count (one, two or three compatible policies) at zero incompatibility, the crossing §9 requires. Two sessions of about 2.5 h, at least 48 h apart. Participants: healthy right-handed adults aged 18–40, normal or corrected acuity and colour vision (hue is a rule feature), no neurological, psychiatric or cardiovascular history or related medication; N follows from the power analysis below. Preregistered exclusions: below 90% accuracy on full-contrast rule trials after training; titrated visibility outside 35–65%; cross-validated decoder AUC below 0.70; over 30% of trials lost to artefact; own ratings failing to order the faces negative < neutral < positive.]{.mark}

[**Stimuli.** Targets are grey-scale faces of six identities (three female) in sad, neutral and happy expressions from the Karolinska Directed Emotional Faces set, whose validation supplies hit-rate, intensity and arousal norms [195]. Sad rather than fearful expressions are used because threat gains access through routes that need not involve valence [128,130]. Sad and happy exemplars are matched on normative arousal; neutral exemplars are necessarily lower, so the critical valence contrast is negative against positive at matched arousal, with trial-wise pupil and skin-conductance responses as covariates in every model. The 18 images are equated on luminance histogram and spatial-frequency spectrum with the SHINE toolbox [196], cropped to an oval, shown at 4°. Each face carries three task features orthogonal to expression: isoluminant tint (three hues), position (above, at or below fixation) and aperture shape (circle, square, diamond).]{.mark}

[**Task and incompatibility manipulation.** Participants overlearn three rules — hue → key, position → key, shape → key — over the same three keys. Instructed rules activate their responses on stimulus presentation without being executed [197,198] — operationally, the content licenses a policy. All three rules hold on every trial, so policy count is three throughout; incompatibility is fixed by the target's feature conjunction: the three demanded keys identical (I1), two identical (I2), or all different (I3). Rule assignment is rotated across participants and every feature value occurs equally often at every level, so stimulus set, salience and task relevance are identical across levels by construction; only the conjunction differs. A trial runs: fixation 500–800 ms; target 33 ms at an individually titrated contrast; pattern mask at 50 ms SOA for 200 ms, staircased to about 50% visibility [69]; 1,000 ms blank. On 25% of trials a probe names one rule and the participant executes it, or presses a fourth key for "no face"; on 75% nothing is required. Probe trials keep the rules active and are the manipulation check, gating the analysis: response time, error rate and lateralised readiness potential must rise monotonically I1 < I2 < I3 at full contrast. The load-control arm (neutral faces) has one or two rules in force, targets chosen so the active rules agree, giving with I1 count one, two, three at zero incompatibility. Trials per cell: 40 in the report session and 64 in the no-report session, over the same eleven cells (nine crossed cells plus two load-control cells; 440 and 704 trials), intermixed in blocks of 44; about 4.5 s per trial, 6 s with the rating.]{.mark}

[**Report-independent signature.** Session 1 adds a Perceptual Awareness Scale rating after the probe phase on every trial [150]; session 2 repeats the stimulation without it. The access index is trial-wise decoded evidence for "seen" from a classifier trained on rating-labelled session-1 trials, using posterior EEG in the 100–350 ms window — where the visual awareness negativity survives removal of report while the P3b does not [103,199–201] — with pupil, blink and microsaccade features — eye measurements of the kind shown to classify perceived from unperceived trials without report [105]. Applied to session-2 no-probe trials, it gives the cell-level dependent variable, mean decoded evidence (logit). Two preregistered conditions guard it: trained on one incompatibility level it must generalise to the others (indexing seeing, not conflict), and it must reproduce session 1's rating-based effects under cross-validation. Its limits: inference from physiology to a conscious state is theoretical, and a no-report participant may still covertly categorise [102,108]; the transfer assumes a seen/unseen signature invariant to report demand, and which windows are invariant is unsettled — the P3b vanishes without report in masking [199] while late sustained activity persists in task-free listening [104]. Every contrast is therefore also computed on the session-1 rating, and a result is claimed only where both arms agree.]{.mark}

[**Cortical and autonomic recording.** 64-channel EEG (0.1–40 Hz; epochs −200 to 800 ms), lead-II ECG, skin conductance and pupil are recorded throughout. The heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP) is the mean fronto-central amplitude 250–400 ms after the last R-peak preceding the target by at least 400 ms, cardiac-field artefact controlled by surrogate-R-peak permutation. Pre-stimulus HEP predicts detection of weak visual [119] and somatosensory stimuli [120] with opposite signs, so its direction is preregistered two-sided; cardiac phase at onset is a covariate. Phasic skin conductance is deconvolved 1–5 s post-target [202,203]; pupil dilation is the baseline-corrected mean 0.5–2 s post-target [204]. The cortical–autonomic coupling index, the operational proxy for M5, is the per-participant, per-cell standardised slope of decoded access on pre-stimulus HEP, and the trial-wise correlation of decoded access with the phasic pupil and skin-conductance responses.]{.mark}

[**Hypotheses as contrasts, and who has predicted what.** C1: linear trend of access over I1 < I2 < I3 at fixed count (weights −1, 0, +1). The M2 column of Table 1 holds one YES, supramodular interaction theory, whose evidence is stronger subjective effects when sustaining incompatible rather than compatible skeletomotor intentions [40]; extending this to access is our derivation, for its proponents to endorse or reject before data are collected. Predictive processing and passive frame theory are IMPLICIT; the other six are NO — silent. A positive C1 therefore confirms one theory and refutes none: GNWT, IIT, RPT, HOT and AST are untouched, because silence is not a contrary prediction. It becomes adversarial for GNWT only if its proponents commit in advance to the null that content-neutral broadcast suggests — what an adversarial preregistration exists to solicit [1]. C2: the load-control trend over count at zero incompatibility; a pure load account predicts a negative slope, no theory a positive one, and C1 is attributable to conflict only if C2 is flat or negative. C3: valence, as (negative + positive)/2 − neutral and as negative − positive at matched arousal. On M3 predictive processing is YES; GNWT, HOT, supramodular interaction theory, passive frame theory and the neural subjective frame are IMPLICIT; the two origin-level accounts are YES at lineage rather than trial level; IIT, RPT and AST are NO. A C3 effect fills an unoccupied cell and discriminates only if GNWT's content neutrality is stated as a null beforehand. C4, the interaction (C1 slope for valenced minus neutral content), and C5, the M5 coupling slope and its change with incompatibility, are unoccupied by all nine trial-level accounts; the companion theory predicts both positive, and both are preregistered as estimation, not confirmation. The genuinely adversarial cell in Table 1 is M4 — supramodular interaction theory's stated negative against the interoceptive-inference YES [39,48] — and this condition does not test it. Testing it needs a report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content: a signature tracking, trial by trial, whether an interoceptive event (a heartbeat, a respiratory load) was accessed, validated against report in a calibration set, generalising to no-report blocks, and dissociable from afferent strength, from arousal and from attention to the body, which alone modulates the HEP [205,206]. None exists; the HEP indexes attention and arousal, not access, and the M4 test waits on its development.]{.mark}

[What each outcome would show follows from the classification in Section 7.4, and we state it
without inflation. A positive effect of incompatibility at matched load corroborates the
supramodular prediction and refutes no silent theory: GNWT, IIT, recurrent processing theory and
the attention schema theory said nothing either way about incompatibility, and silence is not a
contrary prediction. The informative outcome is the null. At adequate power, the absence of an
incompatibility effect with load and salience matched would disconfirm the only stated prediction
over M2 and would leave the arbitration reading of access without its one measured predictor. A
difference in access threshold between matched valenced and neutral content occupies a cell no
coded theory claims, which makes it the cheapest way to convert theory-orphaned evidence into
theory-relevant evidence — again without discriminating between the coded theories. The contested
cell is M4, and it is the only one in Table 1 where a result would count against a stated
prediction whichever way it came out: supramodular interaction theory and passive frame theory
predict no access for a purely autonomic–interoceptive conflict without skeletomotor output, while
interoceptive-inference accounts predict that there is [39] against [48], and the neural subjective frame makes cortical monitoring of visceral input a
precondition of access [116]. The opposition is on record from both
sides; what is not on record is a way of measuring the outcome.]{.mark}

[**Power.** Per-participant contrast scores (paired t, α = 0.05 two-sided), confirmed by trial-level mixed-model simulation with participant random intercepts and slopes [207,208] (`section9_power.py`, `section9_power.csv`), give 90% power at N = 68 for the incompatibility trend at dz = 0.40 and N = 171 at dz = 0.25; for the interaction, N = 119 at dz = 0.30 and N = 265 at dz = 0.20. Trials per cell matter little once participant slopes are heterogeneous: in the two-stage simulation (2,000 runs, N in steps of 10) going from 32 to 64 trials changes the N required by 0–10; the coarser mixed-model grid (200 runs per point) shows differences of 0–35 that are within its Monte Carlo and grid resolution. The 64 no-report trials per cell are set by decoder reliability. The planning values sit below the nearest published effects, all of which retained variance this design removes: incompatible versus compatible intentions on report-based ratings, t(13) = 6.21 [40]; working-memory load on masked-detection d′, d = 0.62–0.86 [209]; cardiac phase on detection, dz ≈ 0.65 [120], with Park et al. [119] as the visual anchor for the HEP–detection link; meta-analytic HEP effects of g ≈ 0.4 [206]. The confirmatory study is set at N = 126 (a multiple of the six rule assignments): 90% power for the trend at dz ≈ 0.29, for the interaction at dz = 0.30, and for equivalence within dz = 0.30 by two one-sided tests [210], so a null on C1 is a supported null; the small interaction is under-powered and reported as estimation. Sequential Bayes-factor monitoring runs from N = 72 in batches of 18 (90, 108, 126) against an interval null [211]; N = 126 is the planned stopping point, and one further batch to a maximum of N = 144 is permitted only if the Bayes factor at N = 126 is still inconclusive.]{.mark}

---

The third condition's design requires care, and we flag the difficulty rather than leave it for a
reviewer, because it is where the prediction could be rendered unfalsifiable in either direction.
Most homeostatic crises recruit skeletal effectors — respiration through the diaphragm and
intercostals, thermoregulation through shivering and posture, so breath-holding is not evidence
against a skeletomotor boundary. What is required is a conflict whose resolution is wholly
autonomic: antagonism between thermoregulatory and gastrointestinal demands with posture fixed and
respiratory pattern change blocked, or orthostatic and thermal loading of the baroreflex without
postural correction. De-efferentation (high spinal injury, locked-in syndrome) is not a discriminator either, since
the claim concerns intentions rather than execution and the central skeletomotor plan is
preserved. Blocking respiratory pattern change is itself a skeletomotor intervention and does not
escape the objection: in an awake participant, skeletomotor intentions survive any fixation of
posture or breathing, and supramodular interaction theory is a claim about intentions.

The deeper obstacle is the one named in Section 7.4: the third condition requires a report-independent
measure of access to interoceptive content, and no such measure has been validated. This is why
we present the third condition as the most discriminating test *in principle* and not as the one
to run first. The order of work is the reverse of the order of interest — the crossed conflict and
valence design uses established visual and auditory no-report measures and can be run now; the
autonomic-only condition needs a validation study developing and calibrating an interoceptive
no-report signature before it can adjudicate anything. [The situation should be stated as it is:
the condition that can be run tests a single-occupant prediction, and the condition that would
discriminate between stated predictions cannot yet be run. That is the actual conclusion of this
review, and it is a smaller claim than an adversarial test on a new quantity — but it names the
measurement whose absence keeps the contested cell closed.]{.mark} Presenting [the third condition]{.mark} as ready would be the kind
of overclaim this review has been criticising.

A closing note on what this review has and has not done. We have not proposed a theory. We have
argued that the field's measurement effort is concentrated on a quantity shared across theories,
documented that concentration by coding, and specified the experiment that would test what the
coding shows to be untested. A theory that does make predictions about the [single-occupant
coordinates]{.mark} —
about the format in which incommensurable local evaluations become comparable, its dimensionality
as a function of the number and heterogeneity of conflicting domains, and organism-wide
reconfiguration breadth as a second condition on access, exists and is set out separately, as
Arbitration Format Theory, in a companion paper. It does not propose an alternative physical basis
for consciousness, and its claims are stated over observables rather than over experience. Whether
it is right is a separate question from whether the [columns are single-occupant. They are so]{.mark} either way,
and the experiment that [tests them]{.mark} is worth running whoever turns out to have predicted the answer.
