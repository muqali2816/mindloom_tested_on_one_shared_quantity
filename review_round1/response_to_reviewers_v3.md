# Response to the Editor and Reviewers

**Manuscript:** brainsci-4583950 — *Brain Sciences* (MDPI)
**Title as submitted:** One quantity, nine theories: what adversarial tests of consciousness are not yet able to measure
**Title as revised:** What theories of consciousness commit to, and what they leave unmeasured: an audit of stated predictions after the first adversarial test
**Decision:** Major revision (23 September 2026)

We thank the Editor and both Reviewers. Reviewer 2's first two points identified an equivocation in the central argument — between a column no theory occupies and a column theories disagree about — and the revision is built around removing it. In removing it we also re-audited our own tables, and that re-audit changed several claims the Reviewers did not challenge; those changes are reported separately in Section 4, because a reviewer should not have to discover them by comparison.

**How to read this document.** Every comment is quoted verbatim, in the order in which it appears in the decision letter (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, then the Editor's own comments, which we place first because they frame the revision), followed by our response, a status and the location of the change in the revised manuscript. Four statuses are used and used strictly:

- **DONE** — the change is in the revised text now.
- **PREPARED** — the material exists (files, scripts, placeholders) but the item awaits an action only the authors can take (a second coding, a deposit, a letter), and the manuscript says so.
- **PARTLY** — part of the request is in the text; the rest is stated as not done, with reasons.
- **DECLINED** — not done, with reasons.

Two kinds of number remain as bracketed placeholders in the text and are never reported here as if they existed: the inter-coder agreement statistics (κ), which depend on a second coding that has not been carried out, and the DOI of the supplementary deposit. Every other count in this document is taken from the fact sheet produced by the reproduction script that accompanies the deposit (`reproduce.py`), not from memory.

**On the highlighting.** The revised manuscript is written in markdown and converted to DOCX and PDF. The yellow highlighting in the PDF is therefore a *reconstructed text comparison* against the submitted version — every passage that was inserted or rewritten is marked — and not Microsoft Word Track Changes. Deleted passages cannot be highlighted; each deletion is listed in Section 5 and at the comment where it is answered. Reviewer line numbers (ll.) refer to the submitted PDF; section numbers below refer to the revised manuscript.

The third block does not equalise precision — the single cells reach about 99 usable no-report trials in session 2 against about 199 in the pooled 1-shared cell — but it removes the risk that a confirmatory cell falls below the 64-trial floor and roughly halves the gap. Block order is a cyclic Latin square over the seven cells with the two extra blocks at the session midpoint and the row reversed in the second half, so that every cell has the same mean position in the session (14 sequences, deposited as `block_orders_study1.csv`). The count branch is estimation: C0 (count 1 against counts 2 and 3) is reported two-sided with its interval, its sign read against P32 (threshold, positive) and P25 (load, negative); the count-3 minus count-2 difference (P33) is reported with the narrowest equivalence bound it satisfies, not tested. **Response.** Accepted in full, and the correction went further than the Reviewer asked, because when we tried to make the distinction operational it changed our own classifications — twice, since an internal re-audit found the first operationalisation still too permissive.

*The distinction is now operational, not rhetorical.* We agree that silence is not a contrary prediction, and the revised analysis separates *whether an account states a prediction* from *whether two predictions can discriminate between accounts*. We built a register of every prediction the coded accounts state over the ten quantities (Table S4 v2: 27 predictions stated by proponents, 6 derived by us and labelled as derived, 1 kept for provenance after re-coding), and a pair register that enters every cross-theory pair of stated predictions within a column — all 63 such pairs — with its shared condition, shared observable and one of four relations: *different* (no shared observable), *jointly compatible*, *discriminating* (a shared observable under a shared condition, with a value predicted by one account and excluded by the other) and *incompatible*. A column's class is then derived by script (`column_typology_pairs.py`) from Table S1 and that register: *contested* only if at least two accounts state predictions and at least one pair is discriminating or incompatible — a difference of locus, timing or terminology alone is not enough; *single-occupant* if exactly one account states a prediction; *occupied-not-contested* if two or more state predictions and no pair discriminates; *thin* if no prediction is stated but at least one account is compatible; *unoccupied* if none. The classes appear in §7.4 as results of the coding, and §1 explains what an experiment on each kind of column can and cannot show. "Silence is not a contrary prediction" is now stated in §1, §7.4 and §9.1.

*What the rule returned, under the current single-coder provisional coding.* M1, M2 and M4b are single-occupant. M3, M4a, M4c, M6 and M7 are occupied-not-contested. M5 is thin. M8 is *contested*, not merely shared — and this overturned what the submitted version said. Six accounts state predictions there; of the 54 cross-theory pairs, 42 are different (a locus claim beside a timing claim is not a disagreement), 10 jointly compatible and 2 discriminating: the GNWT and IIT predictions on the temporal profile of the signature (onset–offset ignition against sustained activity), which Cogitate preregistered and did discriminate, at pair confidence 2; and a recurrent-processing against higher-order pair at confidence 1, which discriminates only if the two sources' outcome variables are one variable. No pair is incompatible outright. Our first revision draft had counted 15 "distinguishable" pairs here and called the column contested at locus, timing and connectivity; that count came from a rule that treated any difference in description as a disagreement, and it is withdrawn — it survives only in `column_typology_v2_legacy_rule.csv` as a diagnostic. M6, which the first draft called "contested at low confidence", is occupied-not-contested: the IIT and higher-order state space statements on dimensionality address different variables, and no shared observable is named. The M4 column — the one the Reviewer and we both prized as the adversarial cell — does not survive as contested once the quantity is stated precisely. We split it into three (Section 4b): M4a, access to interoceptive content as content; M4b, visceral modulation of access to exteroceptive content; M4c, conscious involvement in a purely autonomic conflict. Supramodular interaction theory's and passive frame theory's stated null is a prediction over M4c and the two nulls agree; the interoceptive-inference positive is a prediction over M4a, beside an origins-account positive that it does not contradict; M4b has one stated occupant after the predictive-processing cell was re-coded INTERPRETED on re-reading.
