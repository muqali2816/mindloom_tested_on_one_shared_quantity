# Response to the Editor and Reviewers

**Manuscript:** brainsci-4583950 — *Brain Sciences* (MDPI)
**Original title:** One quantity, nine theories: what adversarial tests of consciousness are not yet able to measure
**Revised title:** Tested on one shared quantity: what adversarial tests of consciousness can and cannot yet measure
**Decision:** Major revision (23 September 2026)

We thank the Editor and both Reviewers. Reviewer 1's first two points identified a genuine equivocation in the central argument — between a column no theory occupies and a column theories disagree about — and the revision is built around removing it. The manuscript's headline result, its title, its abstract and its concluding section have changed accordingly. Every inserted or rewritten passage is highlighted in yellow in the revised manuscript (DOCX and PDF); deleted passages are listed below at the point where they are answered, since a deletion cannot be highlighted.

Two items cannot be completed until the authors act, and we say so plainly rather than fill them in: the second-coder agreement statistic (the second author's blind coding is under way; the codebook, blank forms and agreement script are deposited) and the DOI of the supplementary deposit (the package is assembled; the DOI is issued on upload). Both appear in the text as bracketed placeholders.

Reviewer comments are quoted verbatim. Line numbers refer to the submitted PDF; section numbers refer to the revised manuscript.

**E4.** *"M5 is not empty. Heartbeat-evoked responses have been used to predict conscious detection, and respiratory phase modulates detection thresholds … that argument must be made explicitly, not by omission."*

We agree that the omission was a fault, and the argument is now made explicitly rather than by silence (§7.4, "Thin, not empty: M5", highlighted). We verified the literature: pre-stimulus heartbeat-evoked responses predict visual detection (Park et al., 2014) and somatosensory detection (Al et al., 2020, 2021); respiratory phase aligns perception with neural excitability (Kluger et al., 2021; Grund et al., 2022); heartbeat-evoked responses detect residual consciousness in post-comatose patients (Candia-Rivera et al., 2021). We then added the neural subjective frame proposal (Park and Tallon-Baudry, 2014; Tallon-Baudry et al., 2018; Azzalini et al., 2019) as a coded row of Table S1, since it states an explicit empirical prediction and therefore meets the inclusion rule. Its codes: M4 YES; M5 IMPLICIT; M3, M6, M8 IMPLICIT; M1, M2, M7 NO.

The substantive judgement, argued in the text: these studies show that a cortical response to one visceral signal predicts detection. They do not state a prediction about organism-wide coherence between cortical and autonomic channels as a joint measure — the original authors' own control analyses separate the heartbeat-evoked response from the measured autonomic state. We therefore code the programme IMPLICIT on M5 and say in so many words that a coder who read the heartbeat-evoked response as a cortical–autonomic coherence metric would fill one cell of the column. The sentence "none operationalises it" has been withdrawn and replaced. The column is described throughout as "thin rather than empty" (0 YES, 3 IMPLICIT among the nine trial-level accounts), and M5 has been demoted from the headline to a qualified secondary claim (see R1-3).

**E5.** *"§9 is an idea, not a design. No stimulus set, no task, no specified report-independent signature, no power analysis."*

A new §9.1 (about 1,650 words, all highlighted) specifies one condition completely: a within-subject 3 × 3 crossing of policy incompatibility (three levels, fixed at three concurrently licensed policies, with a neutral load-control arm varying policy count at zero incompatibility) and content valence; the stimulus set (Karolinska Directed Emotional Faces, sad/neutral/happy, matched on normative arousal, equated with SHINE, with three orthogonal task features); the task (three overlearned rules over the same keys, incompatibility fixed by the target's feature conjunction, probe trials as the manipulation check); trial and block counts for a report session and a no-report session; the report-independent signature (trial-wise decoded evidence for "seen" from posterior EEG in the 100–350 ms window plus oculomotor features, trained on Perceptual Awareness Scale-labelled report-session trials and applied to identical stimulation without report, with its known limits stated); simultaneous EEG, ECG (heartbeat-evoked response), skin conductance and pupil recording, with the derived coupling index; preregistered exclusions; and five contrasts, each labelled with which theories predict it and which are silent.

The power analysis (deposited `section9_power.py`, `section9_power.csv`) gives, at α = 0.05 and power 0.90 for the incompatibility trend, N = 68 under a medium effect (dz = 0.40) and N = 171 under a small one (dz = 0.25); for the incompatibility × valence interaction, N = 119 (dz = 0.30) and N = 265 (dz = 0.20). The confirmatory N is set at 126. Effect-size anchors are Morsella et al. (2009a; incompatible versus compatible intentions, an upper bound), Konstantinou and Lavie (2013; load on masked detection) and Al et al. (2020; cardiac phase on detection). We state that the analytic and two-stage simulation estimates agree and that the mixed-model simulation is coarser (Gaussian LMM on a continuous access index, 200 simulations per grid point).

## 2. Reviewer 1

We are grateful for a review that was both severe and exact. Points 1 and 2 are answered first because, as the Reviewer says, they are the substance.

**R1-1.** *"The ms. proceeds as though 'unoccupied' and 'disagreed about' were the same thing. They are not. A column that eight theories are silent on cannot adjudicate between them … Silence is not a contrary prediction … the condition that can be run does not discriminate and the condition that discriminates cannot be run … that is the actual conclusion of the paper and the abstract currently advertises something else."*

Accepted in full. The revision introduces three named kinds of column and uses them consistently in the abstract, §1, §7.4 and §9 (all highlighted):

- *Shared*: several theories state predictions that share the dependent variable (M8). This is what Cogitate tested and why it could challenge many and adjudicate none.
- *Contested*: at least two theories state opposed predictions. Only here does an experiment discriminate. There is exactly one such column, M4 (2 YES against 2 YES(negative) among trial-level accounts).
- *Single-occupant*: one theory or family states a prediction (M1, M2). An experiment varying it corroborates that theory if positive and constrains no silent theory; its informative outcome is the null.
- *Unoccupied / thin*: no stated prediction (M5, with the qualification in E4).

The specific passages the Reviewer cites have been rewritten. The §1 diagnosis (old ll. 49–54) now separates the two senses of "gap" and states what each kind of column can and cannot do. The §9 claim that a conflict-load effect discriminates against GNWT "which predicts none" (old ll. 783–786) has been **deleted**; §9 now concedes that GNWT plus any capacity assumption predicts that competing policies degrade access through load, notes that GNWT makes no prediction about incompatibility at matched load, and explains that the design therefore holds load and salience fixed and varies incompatibility — and that a positive result still refutes no silent theory. The abstract now ends: "The experiment that can be run now tests a single-occupant prediction; its informative outcome is the null." M4 is named in the abstract as the contested column that cannot yet be run for want of a report-independent measure of access to interoceptive content.

**R1-2.** *"Three of the eight columns are the author's own … the sensitivity analysis offered in response tests the wrong proposition … What would carry weight is a column set generated independently of the author. Two routes … Take the dimensional scheme in the ConTraSt database … and code against it. Or write to proponents …"*

We took the first route and name the second as the next step. A new §7.5, "An independently generated column set", reports Table S3: the same accounts coded on the same YES/IMPLICIT/NO scale against the twelve annotation dimensions of the ConTraSt database (type of consciousness; report/no-report; consciousness measure; paradigm; task; stimulus; population; technique; dependent measure; temporal, spatial and frequency findings), with justification and DOI per cell (120 cells).

What the coding shows, stated in the text without advocacy: along the author-external column set no dimension is empty — every one of the twelve carries at least one stated prediction, and seven carry predictions from four or more of the eight neuroscientific theories. So the theories are not uniformly underspecified, and the emptiness of M5 in Table S1 is not an artefact of general vagueness. Second, M1, M2 and M5 correspond to no ConTraSt field: no field counts concurrent action policies, records conflict magnitude, or encodes joint cortical–autonomic coherence (cortical connectivity tags and cardiac tags exist separately). The quantities the manuscript calls unmeasured are also unrecorded in the largest theory-neutral inventory — consistent with our claim, and we say explicitly that it does not demonstrate it, since ConTraSt records what authors reported, not what they could have varied. The limitations of the S3 coding (dimension list reconstructed from the open-access preprint and the live schema; single coder; six YES cells resting on explicit invariance claims, flagged, with strictly differential counts also given) are stated in §7.5. The letter-to-proponents route is named in §7.7 as not done.

We also removed the sentence the Reviewer objected to — that the emptiness of M5 is "for theoretical rather than technical" reasons — and the framing of the drop-M1/M2/M5 sensitivity check as a test of independence.

**R1-3.** *"§7.8 states that the M5 result does not survive the least favorable reclassification … The abstract carries the claim unqualified. One of those two has to change. My preference is that the M1/M2 result … becomes the headline."*

Done as the Reviewer prefers. The title no longer names a count of theories; the abstract leads with the shared/contested/single-occupant distinction and states the M1/M2 result as the robust finding ("no reclassification of borderline cells makes either contested"); M5 appears as "thin rather than empty" with the qualification attached. §7.7 ("What survives reclassification") spells out that M1 and M2 survive promotion of every IMPLICIT cell (M1: 1 YES, 1 IMPLICIT; M2: 1 YES, 2 IMPLICIT — the IMPLICIT cells belong to passive frame theory and predictive processing) while M5 does not.

**R1-4.** *"Tables S1 and S2 were not supplied … the entire evidential content of §7 was unavailable to me."*

See E1. Every cell the Reviewer could not check is now checkable, with a justification and DOI.

**R1-5.** *"I would say necessary now. Seventy-two cells and thirty-six studies is a couple of afternoons for a competent second reader … I am not willing to endorse the coding without it."*

Accepted. The second author is carrying out a blind second coding of all 88 cells of Table S1 and all 36 rows of Table S2 using a codebook written for the purpose (deposited as `codebook.md`: operational definitions of M1–M8, decision rules for the four codes with worked examples drawn from theories outside the coded set, the theory-addressed rule, the content categories, and primary sources per theory — with no first-coder codes or justifications). Agreement is computed by a deposited script (`agreement.py`: unweighted and linearly weighted κ, κ for the binary "stated prediction" question, κ for the IMPLICIT/NO boundary specifically, per-column κ, and a disagreement list for adjudication). One limit on blindness is stated in §7.2 rather than left for the Reviewer to find: the second author had read the submitted manuscript, including its condensed Table 1, so blindness is complete for the per-cell justifications, for the sixteen cells added in revision and for Table S3, and partial for the seventy-two original cells; agreement is reported for the two subsets separately as well as overall. **R1-6.** *"The stated rule does not pick out these nine and no others … UAL and the Feinberg–Mallatt scheme are accounts of when and in which lineages consciousness appeared … Two rows are also compounds … If the sample is purposive, say so … and stop reporting the tallies as fractions of nine."*

**R1-8.** *"The zeros in Table 2 appear only when the denominator drops from 33 to 27, and the category that does the dropping, 'theory-addressed,' is never given an operational definition.
