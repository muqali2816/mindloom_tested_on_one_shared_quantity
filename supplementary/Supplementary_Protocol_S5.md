Power figures are read from `power_study1.csv`, `power_study2.csv` and `power_mixed_check.csv`, written by `section9_power.py` (v2.2, seed 20260924); the session budgets, durations and multiplicity-adjusted power figures of S5.8, S5.13 and S5.15 are written by `protocol_numbers.py` to `protocol_numbers.json`. **Changes from protocol version 1 (summary).** (i) The count branch (C0) and its equivalence half are relabelled as author-derived operationalisations (P32, P33); Study 1 no longer claims to test the stated P16. (ii) The trial budget separates session 1 (titration, rating on every trial, classifier training) from session 2 (the no-report confirmatory data); the earlier figure of 102 presented trials per cell pooled both sessions and left an expected 32.5 usable no-report trials per cell in session 2, not 65. (iii) The block scheme, the probe, the EEG index, the pilot criteria, the autonomic windows and the multiplicity rule are now specified. ## S5.1 Why the earlier design was replaced

The earlier §9.1 varied incompatibility through the target's feature conjunction: three rules demanded three identical keys (I1), two identical and one different (I2) or three different keys (I3), with N = 126 and sequential Bayes-factor monitoring. Under that manipulation the number of distinct responses (1, 2, 3) covaried with incompatibility, so the incompatibility contrast was also a contrast in response-set size and in congruency. The supramodular sources locate the gradation of conscious conflict along the compatibility axis and explicitly allow compatible plans to co-occur without perturbing consciousness (synchrony blindness; Morsella et al. 2016, authors' response), so the earlier design could not have tested what the theory states. The present design fixes the number of distinguishable responses at three and varies only whether they can be executed jointly.

## S5.2 What is tested

Study 1 tests two author-derived operationalisations (P32, P23) of one account's stated commitments and estimates a third quantity. A further derived hypothesis (P33) is bounded by an equivalence test and is not a confirmatory contrast. The condition tests no stated prediction of any coded theory directly, and it discriminates between no two theories. P16 and P25 appear in the table because the design is built from them, but no contrast tests either.

| Label | Prediction | Status | Source | Role in Study 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P16 | Conscious conflict when ≥ 2 *incompatible* inclinations reach the skeletomotor output system; outcome variable experienced conflict; nothing stated beyond two | stated (SIT) | Morsella 2005 (abstract); Morsella et al. 2016 §2.4 | **not tested**: C0 varies the number of *co-executable* policies and measures access to a masked visual target, so it changes both the condition and the outcome that P16 names |
| P32 | Access to a masked target is higher when ≥ 2 co-executable policies are in force than when one is | derived by the authors from P16; not endorsed by SIT proponents | this manuscript | confirmatory contrast C0 |
| P33 | No further increase in access from 2 to 3 co-executable policies | derived by the authors; the source is silent beyond two, and silence is not a stated null | this manuscript | equivalence test C0-eq, reported as a bounded estimate |
| P23 | Access to a masked target rises with the incompatibility of the policies it licenses, at fixed count and load | derived by the authors; not endorsed by SIT proponents | this manuscript | confirmatory contrast C1a |
| P25 | More competing policies degrade access (load) | derived; GNWT plus a capacity assumption GNWT has not made | this manuscript | **not tested**; a monotone decrement over 1/2/3 would be consistent with it and is reported descriptively |
| — | Cortical–autonomic coupling index | estimation only; no coded theory predicts over it | — | estimated with interval |

Why P32 is not P16. P16's condition is competition between incompatible skeletomotor plans and its outcome is experienced conflict. C0 holds all policies co-executable (0 shared effector pairs) and measures access to the target that licenses them. The stated threshold at two inclinations does not entail any function of visual access on the number of compatible responses; the authors assume that transfer (auxiliary assumptions listed in the P32 register row). A null on C0 constrains that derivation, not supramodular interaction theory. Likewise, a null on C1a (P23) constrains the authors' derivation within the equivalence bound to be preregistered; it does not refute the theory, whose stated outcome variable is experienced conflict, not access.

## S5.3 Participants

Healthy right-handed adults aged 18–40, normal or corrected acuity and colour vision (hue is a rule feature), no neurological, psychiatric or cardiovascular history or related medication (design choice, carried from the earlier version). Confirmatory sample N = 120 (119 required for 90 % power at dz = 0.30 for a single uncorrected contrast, rounded up to a multiple of the six rule-to-effector rotations), fixed in advance; no sequential monitoring. The multiplicity-adjusted and conjunctive power at this N are lower and are given in S5.13. Recruitment continues until 120 participants have complete, non-excluded data for both sessions; the number recruited and the number excluded on each criterion are reported.

## S5.4 Stimuli

Targets are grey-scale neutral-expression faces of six identities (three female) from the Karolinska Directed Emotional Faces set [1], equated on luminance histogram and spatial-frequency spectrum with the SHINE toolbox [2], cropped to an oval and shown at 4° (design choices carried from the earlier version). Each face carries three task features orthogonal to identity: isoluminant tint (three hues), position (above, at or below fixation) and aperture shape (circle, square, diamond). Each rule maps its three feature values onto the three positions of a lever (up, middle, down). The stimulus set is restricted to feature conjunctions for which the three demanded lever positions are mutually distinct, so that on every trial the target demands three distinguishable responses; the six permutations of (up, middle, down) over the three rules occur equally often. Rule-to-feature and value-to-position assignments are rotated across participants.

## S5.5 Effectors and the incompatibility manipulation

Three three-position levers: left hand, right hand, dominant foot. A response is a lever position. Two responses on different levers can be executed jointly; two responses on the same lever cannot. Incompatibility on a trial is the number of pairs of demanded responses that share a lever:

| Shared pairs | Effector mapping in force | Co-executable? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | hue → lever A, position → lever B, shape → lever C | all three jointly |
| 1 | two rules on one lever, the third on another | the pair on the shared lever cannot be executed jointly |
| 3 | all three rules on one lever | none jointly |

The mapping is fixed for a block, announced by a block cue at block start (a diagram of the three levers with the rule symbols placed on them, shown for 5 s and available on request during the inter-block break) and overlearned in training; the stimulus set is identical across mappings. The count branch keeps all rules on separate levers (0 shared pairs) and puts one, two or three rules in force.

**Effector identity is rotated within participant where the cell list allows it.** In the 3-shared cell all rules sit on one lever, in the 0-shared cells on three; if the shared lever were fixed for a participant, the incompatibility contrast would also be a contrast between one effector and three (and between hand and foot). Each participant therefore completes the three 1-shared presentation cells with each of the three levers as the shared lever; the single 3-shared cell's lever and the rule-to-lever assignment for the 0-shared cells are rotated across participants in six orders — the rotation count that fixes N at a multiple of six. Cell means are taken over levers, so effector identity is balanced within every analysis cell across participants rather than confounded with incompatibility; any residual lever effect is reported as an exploratory factor.

## S5.6 Cell list and block scheme

Eleven presentation cells, collapsing to five analysis cells:

| Analysis cell | Presentation cells | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| count 1, 0 shared | 3 | which single rule/lever is in force |
| count 2, 0 shared | 3 | which pair of rules/levers is in force |
| count 3, 0 shared | 1 | one mapping (levers rotated across participants) |
| count 3, 1 shared | 3 | which pair of rules shares a lever |
| count 3, 3 shared | 1 | all rules on one lever (lever rotated across participants) |

**Blocks (to be preregistered).** Presentation cells are blocked, not interleaved: a block contains trials of one presentation cell under one fixed mapping. Each session runs 11 cells × 2 blocks = 22 blocks. In session 2 a block has 52 trials (13 probe, 39 no-probe); in session 1 a block has 32 trials (8 probe, 24 no-probe). Block order is a Latin-square sequence over the 11 cells, counterbalanced across participants and reversed between the two blocks of the same cell within a session, so that every cell appears once in each half of the session; the order is fixed at preregistration and not chosen by the experimenter. Each block begins with the block cue of S5.5 and, in session 1, with four full-contrast practice trials under the new mapping that are not analysed. Because the mapping changes only at block boundaries, a participant never has to switch mapping within a block; the cost is that cell and time-in-session are partly confounded within a session, which the two-block-per-cell design and the half-session balance are meant to limit and which the block-wise cross-validation of S5.10 respects.

## S5.7 Trial structure, probe and sessions

Fixation 500–800 ms; target 33 ms at an individually titrated contrast; pattern mask at 50 ms SOA for 200 ms, contrast staircased to about 50 % visibility [3]; 1,000 ms blank; then either the probe phase or nothing; then an inter-trial interval of 2.5–3.5 s, jittered uniformly (design choices; the interval is set by the autonomic windows of S5.11). The planning value is 6 s per trial without the rating and 8 s with it.

**Probe.** On a quarter of trials a probe (the symbol of one rule, 1,000 ms) names one rule in force and the participant executes that rule alone on the appropriate lever, or gives a fourth response (a foot-pedal press) for "no face"; on three quarters of trials nothing is required. The probe names one rule and one response; joint execution of several responses is never required. Probe trials keep the rules active across the block and supply the manipulation check. **Open question, stated plainly:** the design cannot establish that a participant forms three incompatible intentions at target onset rather than holding three rules in memory until the probe selects one. The check that bears on it is probe-response latency as a function of shared pairs (0, 1, 3) at count 3: if intentions are formed at onset, resolving incompatibility at probe time should cost latency in the 1- and 3-shared cells relative to 0-shared; if rules are merely held, latency should not depend on shared pairs. The latency contrast is reported with estimate and interval and is not a confirmatory test; a null on it weakens the intention reading of the manipulation and is reported as such.

**Sessions.** Session 1: contrast titration, training to criterion on the mappings, the full 11-cell procedure with a Perceptual Awareness Scale rating [4] after the probe phase on every trial, and the data on which the classifier is trained. Session 2: the same stimulation without the rating; only session-2 no-probe trials enter the confirmatory no-report estimate. Two sessions at least 48 h apart.

## S5.8 Presented and usable trials (from `protocol_numbers.json`)

The confirmatory no-report estimate uses session-2 no-probe trials only. Session-1 trials all carry a rating and serve titration, classifier training and the rating-based mirror analysis. Budget parameters: probe fraction 0.25, assumed loss 0.15, target ≥ 64 usable no-report trials per presentation cell. The minimum session-2 presentation count for an expected 64 usable trials at these parameters is 101; the plan uses 104 so that the probe count per block is an integer.

| Session | Role | Presented per presentation cell | Blocks × trials | Presented total (11 cells) | Expected trials after loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | titration, rating on every trial, classifier training | 64 (16 probe + 48 no-probe) — a design choice, judged sufficient for a per-participant two-class classifier | 22 × 32 | 704 | 598.4 for training (all rated); 54.4 per cell for the rating-based mirror |
| 2 | no-report confirmatory data | 104 = 26 probe + 78 no-probe | 22 × 52 | 1144 | 66.3 usable no-report per presentation cell (78 × 0.85) |

Analysis cells that pool three presentation cells therefore expect 198.9 usable no-report trials; the count-3/0-shared and count-3/3-shared cells, which are single presentation cells, expect 66.3. The mixed-model check of S5.13 is calibrated to 64 usable trials per analysis cell and so is conservative for the pooled cells and approximately right for the single ones. The 66.3 figure is an expectation, not a guarantee, and the exclusion rule of S5.9 fires when *any* analysis cell falls below 64. Under a simple model of independent trial loss at the planning rate the probability that a single 78-trial cell reaches 64 is 0.8151; for the two single-presentation-cell analysis cells of Study 1 jointly about 0.6644, and for the nine cells of Study 2 about 0.1588 (`protocol_numbers.json`; illustrative, since artefact losses are correlated within a session). The mean alone is therefore not a safe budget. Two provisions are preregistered: (i) a presentation cell that ends session 2 below the floor is completed by one repeated block of that cell at the end of the session (a fixed rule, not a judgement); (ii) the pilot reports the distribution of usable trials per cell, and if the 10th percentile is below the floor after the repeat the per-cell presentation count is raised before preregistration (the theoretical minimum is 110 presented per cell; the smallest budget that keeps an integer number of probe trials in each of the two blocks is 112, which gives approximately a 0.989 chance of reaching the floor under the independent-loss model — a reserve calculation, not a replacement of the 104-trial plan). Participants still below the floor after the repeated block are excluded and replaced up to the fixed N.

**Duration.** Session 2: 1144 trials × 6 s = 114.4 min of trials plus 21 inter-block breaks of 1 min = 135.4 min, plus set-up. Session 1: 704 trials × 8 s = 93.9 min plus 21 min of breaks = 114.9 min, plus titration, training and set-up, for which `[NUMBER: set-up, titration and training time — to be measured in the pilot]`. Both figures exceed the earlier plan (561 presented trials per session) because that plan under-counted the session-2 budget by about half. If the pilot shows that a 135-min recording is not sustainable, session 2 is split into two visits of 11 blocks each under the same frozen classifier; this is a permitted protocol variant to be fixed at preregistration, not a post hoc change.

## S5.9 Exclusions (to be preregistered)

Below 90 % accuracy on full-contrast probe trials after training; titrated visibility outside 35–65 % on the Perceptual Awareness Scale in session 1; cross-validated classifier AUC below 0.70 in session 1 (S5.10, S5.14); more than 30 % of trials lost to artefact in either session; fewer than 64 usable no-report trials in any analysis cell of session 2 (thresholds carried from the earlier version as design choices, the last added in version 2). A participant excluded on any criterion is replaced until N = 120 is reached. The AUC criterion defines the population to which the confirmatory result applies: adults in whom awareness of a masked face is decodable from posterior EEG at AUC ≥ 0.70 under these conditions. The proportion excluded on this criterion is reported as k of n recruited, and the confirmatory conclusion is stated for this population only.

## S5.10 The access index (specification to be frozen)

The access index is the trial-wise decoded evidence (logit) for "seen" from a per-participant classifier trained on session-1 trials labelled by the Perceptual Awareness Scale rating. Every element below is a design choice to be preregistered; the pilot (S5.14) may adjust them before preregistration and nothing may change after it.

- **Channels.** 64-channel EEG in the extended 10–20 layout, 0.1–40 Hz, 500 Hz sampling, average reference after cleaning. The classifier uses the 17 parieto-occipital channels P7, P5, P3, P1, Pz, P2, P4, P6, P8, PO7, PO3, POz, PO4, PO8, O1, Oz, O2. No frontal or central channel enters the index, so that a fronto-central report-related component cannot carry it.
- **Preprocessing.** Bad-channel detection and spherical-spline interpolation; independent component analysis with automated labelling and removal of ocular and cardiac components; epochs −200 to 800 ms around target onset, baseline −200 to 0 ms; rejection of epochs exceeding ±100 µV on any retained channel; downsampling to 100 Hz. The same pipeline, with parameters frozen from session 1, is applied to session 2 without re-fitting.
- **Features and classifier.** Mean amplitude in twenty 20-ms bins from 100 to 500 ms post-target on the 17 channels (340 features), a window that covers the visual awareness negativity, which survives removal of report, and is cut at 500 ms to limit the contribution of the report-dependent P3b, which it cannot exclude entirely [5–8]. Classifier family: linear discriminant analysis with shrinkage regularisation of the covariance [18]. No other family is tried.
- **Labels.** The rating is binarised as 1 ("no experience") against 2–4 (any experience); the binarisation is fixed and is not tuned to maximise separation.
- **Validation and leakage control.** Nested cross-validation on session 1 with block-wise folds: the outer loop leaves one of the 22 session-1 blocks out, so that no trial from the same block (same mapping, same time in session) appears in both training and test; the inner loop selects the shrinkage parameter on the training blocks only. Stimulus identity is balanced across folds. The reported session-1 AUC is the outer-fold average.
- **Transfer condition (index of seeing, not of conflict).** Trained on the blocks of one incompatibility level, the classifier must reach AUC ≥ 0.70 on the blocks of each other level. Rating-based cell differences are reported alongside index-based ones descriptively; reproducing them is not a criterion (S5.14).
- **Freezing.** After session 1 the shrinkage parameter, the channel set, the bins, the preprocessing parameters and the per-participant weights are written to the archive with a hash before any session-2 trial is scored; the frozen files are part of the deposit. The index is EEG-only: no pupil, blink or oculomotor feature enters it, so that the autonomic channels of S5.11 remain independent of it.

The index is a candidate marker of access and not a measure of M5. Every contrast is also computed on the session-1 rating (the PAS-report version); the success rule of S5.12 requires both versions.

## S5.11 Autonomic channels and the coupling metric

Lead-II ECG, skin conductance and pupil diameter are recorded throughout both sessions alongside the EEG. Timing markers are written to all channels at fixation onset, target onset, probe onset, response and inter-trial-interval onset. The inter-trial interval of 2.5–3.5 s (S5.7) is set so that the response windows below are complete before the next fixation on most trials, but at a planning value of 6 s per trial the skin-conductance response to one target overlaps the next trial; overlapping responses are therefore separated by deconvolution rather than by lengthening the interval.

- **Heartbeat-evoked potential.** Mean fronto-central amplitude 250–400 ms after the last R-peak preceding the target by at least 400 ms, cardiac-field artefact controlled by surrogate-R-peak permutation; direction two-sided [9,10]; cardiac phase at onset as covariate.
- **Skin conductance.** Phasic driver recovered by continuous decomposition analysis [11,12], which models the overlapping responses of successive trials; the trial response is the integrated driver 1–5 s after target onset.
- **Pupil.** Baseline-corrected mean diameter 0.5–2.5 s after target onset, with a general-linear-model deconvolution of the pupil trace using target onsets and probe onsets as regressors to separate the response to the current target from the tail of the previous one and from the probe [13].

Coupling index: (a) the per-participant, per-cell standardised slope of decoded access on pre-stimulus heartbeat-evoked amplitude; (b) the trial-wise correlation of decoded access with the phasic skin-conductance and pupil responses. Both are reported with point estimate and 95 % interval per cell and as a function of shared pairs. **Estimation only:** no coded theory predicts over the index, it is not a measure of M5 in the organism-wide sense of §7, and no hypothesis test is attached to it.

## S5.12 Contrasts, multiplicity and the success rule

Effect sizes are dz on per-participant contrast scores of the cell-mean access index; α = 0.05 two-sided.

| Contrast | Cells (order) | Weights | Test | Hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C0 | count 1, 2, 3 at 0 shared | −1, 0.5, 0.5 | paired t | P32 (derived) | confirmatory |
| C1a | shared 0, 1, 3 at count 3 | −1, 0.5, 0.5 | paired t | P23 (derived) | confirmatory |
| C0-eq | count 2, 3 at 0 shared | 0, −1, 1 | TOST, bounds ±0.30 dz, α 0.05 each side [14] | P33 (derived) | bounded estimate |
| C1b-lin | shared 0, 1, 3 at count 3 | −1.3333, −0.3333, 1.6667 (linear in shared pairs) | paired t | — | exploratory |
| C1b-omni | shared 0, 1, 3 at count 3 | two orthogonal contrasts | Hotelling T², F(2, N − 2) | — | exploratory |

**Multiplicity (to be preregistered).** There are two confirmatory contrasts, C0 and C1a. Their p-values are corrected by the Holm step-down procedure at family-wise α = 0.05 [19]. C0-eq is a secondary equivalence test with pre-fixed bounds, outside the confirmatory family: it can support P33 only by TOST significance, never by the absence of an ordinary significant difference. C1b is exploratory and is not reported as a test of any hypothesis. Ordinal weights (−1, 0, +1) are not used for C1b because they would treat the step from 1 to 3 shared pairs as equal to the step from 0 to 1. A null on C1a is interpreted through a TOST with the same ±0.30 dz bound.

**Success rule (to be preregistered).** The rule is stated per contrast (rule A). A confirmatory contrast is counted as supported only if (i) its EEG-index version is significant after Holm correction *in the direction predicted* — an increase of access with count for C0 (P32) and with incompatibility for C1a (P23); a significant effect in the opposite direction is a disconfirmation, not a pass — and (ii) its PAS-report version, computed on the session-1 ratings (about 54.4 rated trials per presentation cell, fewer than the 66.3 no-report trials of session 2, so this version is the less powerful of the two), is significant after the same correction and has the same, predicted, sign. A contrast that passes on one version only is reported as unsupported, with both estimates. Each contrast is judged on its own; the study does not require both contrasts to pass, and we report the power of that stricter joint reading in S5.13 so that a reader who prefers it can see its cost. The rule is conjunctive across the two versions and its power is lower than the power of either version alone (S5.13).

Fixed sample N = 120: achieved power 0.9032 at dz = 0.30 for C0 and for C1a taken singly and uncorrected; minimum detectable dz at 90 % power 0.2983.

**Multiplicity-adjusted and conjunctive power (from `protocol_numbers.json`).** Under Holm with two hypotheses, the power of each contrast singly is at least its power at the Bonferroni level α = 0.025, which at N = 120 and dz = 0.30 is 0.8438 (an exact lower bound that needs no independence assumption); the Holm step-down adds the cases in which the other contrast is rejected first, giving 0.8939 if the two p-values were independent — an approximation, since C0 and C1a share the count-3/0-shared cell. For the success rule (rule A: one contrast, both versions) the EEG-index and PAS-report versions must both pass. Their joint power cannot be derived analytically without the correlation between the two versions, which is unknown before the pilot; under independence it is the product, 0.8438² = 0.712 at the Bonferroni level and 0.8939² = 0.799 under the Holm approximation — and the PAS version, resting on the smaller session-1 trial count, is treated here as equally powered, which overstates rule A's power by an amount the pilot will show. Two stricter readings are reported for completeness and are not the preregistered rule: both contrasts passing in one modality has power 2ab − a² = 0.8122 (a = 0.8438, b = 0.9032), and all four tests passing has power about 0.6596 under independence. The two versions are measured on the same participants and stimuli and are expected to be positively dependent, which would raise every joint figure above its product, but by an amount `[to be computed from the pilot correlation]`. For 90 % power at the Bonferroni level a single contrast would need N = 141, and rule A under independence N = 170. The fixed N = 120 is therefore adequately powered for each contrast singly and uncorrected, close to 90 % after Holm correction, and not adequately powered for rule A under independence; the rule is retained because it protects against a decoder artefact, and its cost in power is stated rather than hidden.

**Exploratory C1b at N = 120** (uncorrected): linear-in-shared-pairs power 0.5844 (dz 0.20), 0.9032 (0.30), 0.9915 (0.40); N for 90 % 265 / 119 / 68. Two-df omnibus power 0.4762 / 0.8359 / 0.979; N for 90 % 320 / 144 / 83.

**Equivalence (C0-eq, P33) at N = 120, true effect 0:** bounds ±0.20 dz, power 0.406, N for 90 % 272; bounds ±0.30, power 0.895 (Monte Carlo 0.896), N for 90 % 122; bounds ±0.40, power 0.993, N for 90 % 70. The ±0.30 bound is the planning effect size itself, stated in units of the population SD; it is not to be replaced by a fraction of the SD estimated on the final data. The equivalence claim is close to 90 % only for that bound or wider, and if the P32 threshold leaves a small residual increase from 2 to 3 the equivalence power falls.

**Mixed-model check (`power_mixed_check.csv`).** At N = 120 and 64 usable trials per cell (the calibration target; the session-2 budget expects 66), 200 simulated data sets calibrated to dz = 0.30 — C1a: analytic 0.903, two-stage paired t 0.935, linear mixed model with random slopes 0.935 (Monte Carlo SE about 0.021), 0 non-converged; C0: 0.903 / 0.900 / 0.900, 0 non-converged. Agreement with the analytic value is expected by construction; the check confirms the calibration and is not an independent estimate. The two-df omnibus power assumes the whole effect lies along one contrast direction.

**Effect-size anchor:** incompatible against compatible intentions on report-based ratings, t(13) = 6.21 [15], an upper bound that retained variance this design removes; the planning value dz = 0.30 sits below it.

## S5.14 Mandatory pilot

A pilot of `[NUMBER: pilot sample size — to be set by the authors]` participants runs the full session-1 procedure and a shortened session 2 of `[NUMBER: blocks]`. Three criteria, each to be preregistered, must all be met before the confirmatory sample begins:

1. **Threshold.** The masking staircase holds Perceptual Awareness Scale visibility within 35–65 % across the session for every included pilot participant, and the titrated contrast is stable across blocks.
2. **Measurement validity.** The classifier specified in S5.10, trained on the blocks of one incompatibility level, reaches cross-validated AUC ≥ 0.70 on the blocks of each of the other two levels, in at least `[NUMBER: k of n pilot participants]`. This criterion tests whether the index measures seeing rather than conflict; it does not require the index to reproduce any cell difference, so it cannot select participants for the hypothesised effect. It does select participants whose awareness is decodable at this accuracy, and that selected population is the one named in S5.9 to which the confirmatory result applies.
3. **Accuracy on the probed rule.** Full-contrast accuracy on the single probed rule ≥ 90 % in every presentation cell, including the 3-shared cell, in which the probed rule must be executed alone on the shared lever. Joint execution is not required by the probe and is not a criterion. The pilot also reports probe-response latency by shared-pairs level (S5.7) descriptively, as the first evidence on whether the manipulation produces simultaneous intentions.

If any criterion fails the study stops and the pilot is reported. If the probe fraction, the loss rate or the trial duration observed in the pilot differs from the planning values, the presented-trial counts and session durations are re-derived by `protocol_numbers.py` before preregistration.

## S5.15 Study 2 — valence (Supplementary only)

**Rationale and status.** Study 2 asks whether valenced content shifts the access threshold at matched arousal, the cell that Table 2 shows to be empty in the checked theory-addressed sample, and whether the incompatibility effect of Study 1 differs by valence. The first question is a confirmatory test of P34, an author-derived hypothesis. It is not a test of the stated P13: P13 (Seth 2013; Joffily & Coricelli 2013) concerns experienced valence and its relation to the rate of change of free energy, and says nothing about the direction of access differences between sad and happy faces at matched arousal. Deriving P34 from P13 needs three auxiliary assumptions, listed in the P34 register row, none of which the source states; a result on P34 corroborates or constrains the authors' derivation and not predictive processing. The second question is estimation.

**Stimuli.** The Study 1 identities in sad, neutral and happy expressions from the same set, whose validation supplies hit-rate, intensity and arousal norms [1]. Sad rather than fearful expressions are used because threat gains access through routes that need not involve valence [16,17]. Sad and happy exemplars are matched on normative arousal; neutral exemplars are necessarily lower, so the critical contrast is negative against positive at matched arousal. Equated with SHINE as in Study 1.

**Pupil and skin conductance: manipulation checks, covariates only with justification.** The trial-wise pupil and skin-conductance responses serve first as manipulation checks that the normative arousal matching holds in these participants: a difference in either between sad and happy trials is reported and, if present, the matching has failed for that participant, who is `[to be decided at preregistration: excluded or flagged]`. They are not entered as covariates in the primary analysis, because a valenced face may change pupil size and skin conductance as a consequence of the manipulation itself; adjusting for a consequence of the manipulation would remove part of the effect under test. They enter only a sensitivity analysis, reported alongside the primary one, whose purpose is to show how much of any negative − positive difference survives adjustment for residual arousal.

**Design.** Nine cells: valence (negative, neutral, positive) × shared pairs (0, 1, 3) at count 3 (the count branch is not repeated); blocks, probe, sessions and budgets as in Study 1 — session 1 with 64 presented trials per cell (18 blocks × 32; 576 trials; about 93.8 min with breaks) and session 2 with 104 per cell (18 blocks × 52; 936 trials; about 110.6 min with breaks), 1512 presented trials per participant in all. Access index, autonomic channels and exclusions as in Study 1, with one added exclusion — a participant whose own post-session ratings fail to order the faces negative < neutral < positive is excluded.

**Sample and power (from `power_study2.csv` and `protocol_numbers.json`).** Confirmatory contrast: negative − positive at matched arousal, paired t, dz = 0.30 → N = 119 at 90 % power (80 %: 90); dz = 0.20 → 265 (80 %: 199); dz = 0.40 → 68 (80 %: 52). Study 2 sample N = 120 (119 required; rounded to a multiple of the six rule-to-effector rotations, as in Study 1), fixed, no sequential monitoring; power for the single contrast at that N is 0.9032 uncorrected (one confirmatory contrast, so no multiplicity correction is needed), and under the same EEG-plus-PAS success rule (rule A) the power is 0.8157 if the two versions were independent (see S5.13 for the status of that assumption). Incompatibility × valence interaction (contrast-of-contrasts): a confirmatory test at dz = 0.20 would need N = 265; at N = 120 its power is 0.584 at dz = 0.20 and 0.903 at dz = 0.30. **The interaction is therefore estimated and reported with its estimate and interval, not tested.** Minimum detectable dz for any paired contrast at N = 120 is 0.2983.

**Interpretation.** A negative − positive effect corroborates the authors' derivation P34 from the one trial-level account that predicts over M3, and separates none of the coded theories: GNWT, higher-order thought theory, supramodular interaction theory and passive frame theory carry constructed nulls over valence that none has stated. The interaction estimate will be reported for any theory that later states a prediction over it.
